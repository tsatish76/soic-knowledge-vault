## Part A: 3-Minute Summary Reference Card (D2)

* **Core Thesis:** Working capital is where paper profits turn into cold cash or vanish into customer defaults. A company growing 25% on paper will go bankrupt if receivables grow 50%. The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) reveals whether a company has distribution bargaining power over its suppliers and dealers.

## Part B: Detailed Lecture Report (D1)

### [00:00] Stage 1: The Working Capital Illusion
The speaker contrasts accounting net profit with cash flow from operations. Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. Many Indian mid-caps showed 30% PAT CAGR between 2015 and 2019 but generated zero cumulative free cash flow because working capital absorbed all operating profits.

### [14:22] Stage 2: The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Formula

$$\text{CCC (Days)} = \text{DIO (Inventory)} + \text{DSO (Receivables)} - \text{DPO (Payables)}$$

* **DIO (Days Inventory Outstanding):** How many days raw materials and finished goods sit in warehouses.
* **DSO (Days Sales Outstanding):** How many days customers take to pay their bills.
* **DPO (Days Payable Outstanding):** How many days the company takes to pay its vendors.

::: tip Rule of Thumb
A negative or near-zero CCC means the business is funded entirely by suppliers and customers (e.g. D-Mart, FMCG).
:::

### [28:10] Stage 3: Case Study — Astral Ltd vs Competitors
The speaker analyzes Astral’s CPVC pipes business. While typical pipe companies extended 60–90 day credit to distributors:
1. Astral demanded advance payments or 15-day strict credit because plumbers demanded Astral brand specifically.
2. Result: Astral’s CCC stayed between 30–38 days throughout 2012–2022.
3. This enabled Astral to fund its entire manufacturing expansion into adhesives and bathware through internal cash accruals without equity dilution.

### [54:30] Stage 4: Channel Stuffing & Working Capital Traps
How to spot channel stuffing before quarterly earnings report a disaster:
* Q4 revenue spikes 40% while Q1 revenue collapses.
* Trade receivables growth rate significantly exceeds revenue growth rate.
* Cash flow from operations turns negative despite record operating margins.

## Self-Test Q&A for Active Recall

::: details Q1: What happens to Free Cash Flow if revenue grows 20% but receivables grow 45%?
Free Cash Flow turns sharply negative because the uncollected revenue must still be funded with working capital, forcing the company to borrow short-term debt or burn cash reserves.
:::

::: details Q2: Why did Astral have bargaining power over its distributor network?
Because Astral created consumer pull through plumber training programs and brand marketing. Distributors could not afford not to stock Astral pipes.
:::

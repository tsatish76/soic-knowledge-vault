### a) What this stage answers

Why does buying high-quality companies at any price (BAP investing above 50x–60x P/E) fail to beat the Nifty 50 over a 5-year cycle, and what starting PEG and P/E thresholds statistically generate positive 3-year alpha?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Two Fundamental Investing Mistakes & The BAP ("Buy at Any Price") Trap

In equity investing, mistakes fall into two distinct categories [[00:05:32–00:06:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=332)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript]:

1. **Buying Frothy / Governance-Flawed Companies:** Investing in frothy businesses where corporate governance issues or outright fraud occur, resulting in permanent **loss of capital** [[00:05:32–00:06:04](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=332)].  
2. **Buying a Good Company at Any Price (BAP Investing):** Paying 50x–100x P/E for high-quality companies under the 2018–2019 belief that quality alone guarantees superior returns at any valuation [[00:03:34–00:06:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=214)]. While capital loss may be avoided, buying a basket at **50x–60x P/E** (roughly **3x the Nifty 50 average P/E of 20x**) exposes the portfolio to **valuation de-rating** whenever earnings growth slows slightly, resulting in underperformance against a simple Nifty 50 index fund [[00:06:04–00:10:35](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=364)].

Speaker's view: Apart from rare outliers (such as Avenue Supermarts / DMart expanding from 100x to 250x P/E, the Adani Group, or optically high P/Es caused by one-off costs in MCX and Honasa Consumer ["HUNASA"]), a stock trading at **80x P/E does not re-rate to 800x P/E**—making starting valuations critical, especially if an investor holds a disproportionate allocation in a high-P/E stock that suffers a de-rating event [[00:10:01–00:11:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=601)].

#### Risk-Averse PEG \< 2x Portfolio Architecture

A simple, repeatable starting-valuation discipline—buying established leaders (minimum market cap \~₹4,000 crore, zero SME or 100x–200x P/E exposure) across diversified sectors at **PEG \< 2.0x** (e.g., 30x–35x P/E with matching growth, or 12x–13x P/E and 3x–3.5x P/B) and sitting patiently—makes portfolio-level loss of capital very difficult [[00:12:10–00:16:11](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=730)].

#### The 2018 Wealth Creation Study: PEG Ratio & Starting P/E Alpha

Citing the **2018 Motilal Oswal ("Motila Lospal") Wealth Creation Study** (covering \~4,000 historical instances, alongside SOIC's internal 5,000–6,000 instance Ace Equity study) [[00:16:49–00:22:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1009)]:

- **PEG Ratio Formula:** Calculated as **Trailing Twelve Months (TTM) P/E ÷ Forward 1-Year Earnings Growth %** (e.g., 20x P/E with 20% growth \= **1.0x PEG**; 30x P/E with 20% growth \= **1.5x PEG**) [[00:16:49–00:18:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1009)].  
- **Hard Rule on PEG (≤ 1.5x, or ≤ 2.0x for Consumer):** Across market cycles (including 2000–2005 and 2008–2013, where broad 1-year market drawdowns temporarily hit returns before patience rewarded holders), sticking below **1.5x PEG** consistently generates positive 3-year alpha over the index [[00:18:20–00:21:23](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1100)]:  
  - **0.0x–0.5x PEG:** \+26% average 3-year alpha (and \+19% across low-PEG cohorts [? verify]).  
  - **0.5x–1.0x PEG:** \+11% average 3-year alpha.  
  - **1.0x–1.5x PEG:** \+3% average 3-year alpha.  
  - **1.5x–2.0x PEG:** \-3% average 3-year alpha (and deeper negative alpha at **2.0x–3.0x** and **3.0x+** PEG, such as paying 50x–60x P/E for 10%–15% growth) [[00:18:20–00:20:44](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1100)].  
  - **Consumer Exception (Up to 2.0x PEG):** Stretching up to **1.66x–2.0x PEG** is permissible *only* for highly predictable consumer businesses (e.g., paying 50x P/E for 30% earnings growth \= **1.66x–1.67x PEG** in Varun Beverages) [[00:19:29–00:20:14](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1169)].  
- **Starting P/E Sweet Spot (30x–40x vs. \>50x–75x):** Buying richly valued stocks at **P/E ≥ 50x does not work** statistically; paying **75x+ P/E delivers \-8% [? verify: spoken as "minus 80, minus 8%"] average 3-year alpha**, whereas **30x–40x P/E** (with 50x as a rare ceiling) is the sweet spot that compounds alpha over long horizons [[00:21:23–00:23:23](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1283)].

#### Thomas Phelps's "Flattened Lemon" & Discounted Information

High P/E multiples (\>50x) usually signal that optimistic information is already priced in [[00:22:34–00:23:23](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1354)]. Quoting **Thomas Phelps** (*100 to 1 in the Stock Market*): *"A lemon that has been flattened by a steam roller has more juice in it than a piece of information the stock market has already discounted"* [[00:23:23–00:25:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1403)]. When a theme (such as Railways, Defence, or Electronic Manufacturing Services / EMS) is on every news channel and Twitter handle, the easy optimism is already discounted in the price, requiring strict exit strategies to convert paper profits into real profits [[00:24:17–00:25:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1457)].

## CC-Lec07 – Stage 2: Capital Cycle, Multiples Quiz, Tailwinds & Intrinsic vs. Transactional P/E [[00:25:34–00:59:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1534)] [TVGP: Valuation]

### a) What this stage answers

How does the capital cycle (QIPs, IPOs, and Capex deferrals) drive sectoral mean reversion, what fundamental drivers govern P/E, P/B, P/S, and EV/EBITDA re-rating, and how do regulatory shocks, growth stalls, and corporate governance structures impact Intrinsic P/E versus Transactional P/E?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Horace's Epigraph, Benjamin Graham & The Capital Cycle ("Capital is a Living Thing")

Quoting Horace's *Ars Poetica* from the epigraph of Benjamin Graham and David Dodd's *Security Analysis*—*"Many shall be restored that now are fallen, and many shall fall that are now in honor"*—neither abnormally good conditions (e.g., EMS stocks at 140x–150x P/E) nor abnormally bad conditions (e.g., banks in 2018 or chemical companies today) last forever [[00:25:34–00:27:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1534)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript].

Drawing from the book *Capital Returns*, **capital is a living thing** that flows toward hyper-optimism and flees near-term pessimism [[00:27:34–00:29:02](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1654)]:

1. **Expansion & Euphoria Phase:** High returns make investors optimistic → companies raise easy money via **QIPs (Qualified Institutional Placements)**, mainboard/SME **IPOs**, and aggressive private capacity additions → rising competition drives returns below the cost of capital → share prices underperform [[00:28:17–00:32:26](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1697)].  
2. **Contraction & Consolidation Phase:** Business investment declines, IPOs and Capex are deferred, and industry consolidates as investors turn deeply pessimistic → improving supply side lifts returns above the cost of capital → share prices outperform [[00:28:17–00:31:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1697)].

Tracking where capital is flooding in (QIPs/IPOs) versus drying up (Capex/IPO deferrals) equips an investor to practice both momentum investing and mean-reversion investing [[00:30:22–00:31:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1822)].

#### Fundamental Determinants & Mismatches Across Four Multiples

From Aswath Damodaran's *The Little Book of Valuation* (where 4 of the 13 chapters are practical for investors, while CAPM/Beta can be skipped)  [[00:33:14–00:39:05](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1994), [00:59:02–00:59:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3542)]:

- **P/E Ratio:** Driven by **expected earnings growth**, higher dividend payout (sustainability), and **lower risk** (e.g., winning a confirmed 2–3 year order book reduces risk and increases visibility). **Mismatch:** Low P/E relative to sector peers while expected growth inflects upward  [[00:33:14–00:34:00](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1994), [00:36:53–00:37:40](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2213)].  
- **Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio:** Driven by expected growth, higher payout, lower risk (deleveraging), and **higher Return on Equity (ROE)** (driven by margin expansion). **Mismatch:** Low P/B relative to the sector while ROE inflects sharply upward  [[00:34:00–00:35:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2040), [00:37:40–00:38:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2260)].  
- **Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio:** Used for **cyclical businesses** and governed by **operating/net margins**. **Mismatch:** Low P/S ratio when margins or capital employed turnover begin improving  [[00:35:20–00:35:30](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2120), [00:38:24–00:39:05](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2304), [00:41:25–00:42:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2485)].  
- **EV/EBITDA:** Driven by higher expected growth, **falling reinvestment rate** (shifting from heavy greenfield expansion to brownfield/utilization), lower risk, **rising ROIC/ROCE**, and lower tax rates—exemplified by hospital stocks post-COVID [[00:35:30–00:36:53](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2130)].

#### Charlie Munger's Tailwinds Principle (*Damn Right\!*, p. 171\)

Citing Janet Lowe's biography of Charlie Munger (*Damn Right\!*, page 171): *"One of the lessons your management has learned—and unfortunately sometimes relearned—is the importance of being in businesses where tailwinds prevail rather than headwinds"* [[00:39:05–00:40:16](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2345)]. Like an airplane flying with a tailwind, when a sector enters tailwinds and growth rates, margins, or capital turnover improve, both P/E and P/S multiples re-rate upward [[00:39:43–00:42:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2383)].

#### Intrinsic Value P/E vs. Transactional (Sentiment) P/E

Every stock has two distinct P/E multiples [[00:42:10–00:46:44](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2530)]:

- **Intrinsic Value P/E:** Grounded in the business's true cash-flow generating power, earnings growth, and discounted present value  [[00:42:10–00:42:42](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2530), [00:44:32–00:45:13](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2672)].  
- **Transactional P/E:** Driven by market sentiment, news flow, emotion, and how much institutional/retail capital wants to buy the stock  [[00:42:10–00:42:42](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2530), [00:44:32–00:45:13](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2672)].  
- **Impact of Government/Regulatory Interference:** When government regulation or adverse policy news hits a sector, **Transactional P/E de-rates immediately** on sentiment (even if intrinsic cash flows remain intact or tied up in court for 3–4 years), and in severe cases **both Transactional and Intrinsic P/E get impaired** [[00:42:42–00:45:13](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2562)]. Conversely, removing promoter share pledging at 12x–13x P/E immediately boosts Transactional P/E [[00:51:34–00:52:22](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3094)].

#### High-P/E Growth Stalls & DII Investment Committee Governance Filters

- **2–3 Year Growth Stalls at High P/E:** When a stock trading at 55x–81x P/E on peak cyclical realizations faces **2 years of flat revenue** due to delayed Capex/land acquisition, the market immediately initiates a P/E de-rating and painful time/price correction even before trailing earnings fall [[00:46:44–00:51:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2804)].  
- **Mutual Fund Investment Committees & Capital Misallocation / Group Structure:** Every domestic AMC/mutual fund has an **Investment Committee** that vets pros and cons before buying [[00:53:59–00:54:41](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3239)]. If a promoter misallocates capital into an unrelated business (e.g., a chemical company opening a hotel), siphons processing charges to a subsidiary, or maintains two separately listed overlapping group entities, **DII investment committees reject the stock**—capping its Transactional P/E at a persistent discount to clean-governance peers until the structure is simplified [[00:52:22–00:58:30](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3142)].  
- **Recommended Valuation Books:** *The Book of Value* (Anurag Sharma), *Expectations Investing* (Michael Mauboussin), *Narrative and Numbers* (Aswath Damodaran), *The Little Book of Valuation* (Aswath Damodaran — focus on 4 core chapters), and *A Short History of Financial Euphoria* (John Kenneth Galbraith) [[00:58:30–00:59:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3510)].

## CC-Lec07 – Stage 3: Bull, Bear & Base Scenario Modeling: RACL Geartech & Technocraft Industries [[00:59:24–01:46:54](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3564)] [TVGP: Growth]

### a) What this stage answers

How can an investor combine company narratives (concall guidance, credit rating reports, and capital allocation shifts) with simplified P\&L math (Capex depreciation, incremental debt interest, and conservative exit P/E multiples) to build Bull, Base, and Bear valuation scenarios and quantify Margin of Safety?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Joining Numbers, Narratives & Stage Analysis ("Roughly Right vs. Engineer's Precision")

Every stock evaluation rests on three pillars: **Numbers**, **Narratives**, and **Stage Analysis** (technical entry/exit timing, deferred to Stage 6\) [[01:00:04–01:00:47](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3604)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript]:

1. **Numbers:** Operating margins vs. sector peers, planned Capex ("Kpex"), Return on Capital Employed (ROCE), asset turnover, and order-book inflows [[01:00:04–01:01:32](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3604)].  
2. **Narratives:** Customer quality (e.g., supplying to global OEMs), niche product positioning, promoter pedigree, and capital allocation shifts toward higher-margin value-added segments  [[01:01:32–01:02:44](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3692), [01:18:58–01:26:29](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4738)].

**Speaker's view on Valuation Precision:** Any valuation model you build will be inherently flawed—some models are simply less biased than others  [[01:02:44–01:03:21](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3764), [01:10:06–01:10:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4206)]. Investors with an engineering, CA, or accounting background often fall into the trap of chasing decimal-point precision ("financial engineering"), which fails in real-world markets where sector sentiment shifts constantly  [[01:06:23–01:07:05](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3983), [01:09:20–01:10:06](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4160), [01:36:26–01:37:19](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5786), [01:41:00–01:41:38](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6060)]. Citing **Benjamin Graham's weight analogy**, you do not need to know a person's exact weight to tell whether they are overweight or thin (e.g., roughly 70–80 kg); similarly, scenario modeling is meant to make you **roughly right** on whether a stock offers a margin of safety  [[01:41:00–01:41:38](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6060), [01:43:36–01:44:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6216)]. In the SOIC valuation template, retail investors only need to populate the **green input cells**  [[00:59:24–01:00:04](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3564), [01:40:37–01:41:22](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6037)].

#### Step-by-Step P\&L Scenario Forecasting Mechanics

To project future Market Cap and expected CAGR over a 3-to-4-year horizon from management guidance and credit rating reports  [[01:03:21–01:14:45](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3801), [01:27:00–01:33:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5220)]:

- **Top-Line (Sales) Projection:** Anchor near-term revenue growth to management concall guidance, TV interviews, or credit rating reports, and cross-check multi-year growth against **planned Capex × Asset Turnover**  [[01:02:10–01:03:49](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3730), [01:20:53–01:22:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4853)].  
- **EBITDA Projection:** Multiply projected sales by sustainable **EBITDA margin %** (adjusting upward by 1% only if backed by higher gross-margin order wins, or keeping flat at guided levels to stay conservative)  [[01:03:49–01:04:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3829), [01:29:31–01:30:42](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5371)].  
- **Depreciation Estimation (Two Practical Shortcuts):**  
  - *Method 1 (Capex ÷ Asset Life):* Divide annual incremental Capex by an assumed **10-to-15-year plant life** (e.g., ₹50 crore annual Capex ÷ 10 years \= **\+₹5 crore incremental depreciation per year**)  [[01:04:25–01:05:48](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3865), [01:09:20–01:10:06](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4160)].  
  - *Method 2 (% of Sales Ratio):* Keep **Depreciation ÷ Sales %** constant at the base-year ratio (e.g., $\text{₹}64\text{ Cr} \div \text{₹}1,985\text{ Cr} = 3.22%$), so projected sales growth automatically scales depreciation in line with the new Capex [[01:27:00–01:30:06](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5220)].  
- **Interest Cost Estimation:** Compare planned Capex \+ working capital needs against internal cash accruals. Multiply incremental debt required by a standard Indian borrowing rate of **\~9% cost of debt** (or step up interest expense by **5%–5.5% per annum** during the heavy Capex phase) and add to base interest expense  [[01:05:48–01:08:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3948), [01:30:42–01:32:15](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5442), [01:35:23–01:36:26](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5723)].  
- **Other Income, PBT & PAT:** Hold recurring export incentives flat or grow cash-backed other income modestly (e.g., 10% p.a.); compute $\text{PBT} = \text{EBITDA} + \text{Other Income} - \text{Depreciation} - \text{Interest}$, and deduct **\~25% corporate tax** ($\text{PAT} = \text{PBT} \times (1 - 0.25)$)  [[01:07:58–01:10:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4078), [01:14:04–01:14:45](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4444), [01:32:15–01:32:40](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5535)].  
- **Terminal Market Cap, Share Price & Expected Return (CAGR):**  
  - Multiply terminal-year PAT by an **Exit P/E multiple** tailored to each scenario (Bull \= re-rated or 3-year average P/E; Base \= current/conservative P/E without re-rating; Bear \= de-rated P/E)  [[01:11:38–01:15:11](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4298), [01:33:51–01:36:26](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5631)].  
  - Divide projected Market Cap by **total shares outstanding** to obtain target price per share [[01:41:38–01:43:36](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6098)].  
  - Compute expected return CAGR using Excel's `RATE(nper, pmt, pv, fv)` function, entering current Market Cap as a **negative value (`-Current Market Cap`)** in `pv` so Excel recognizes it as an initial cash outflow  [[01:32:40–01:33:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5560), [01:44:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6260)].

#### The Fundamental Margin of Safety Rule (Base Case \> 15% CAGR)

Because an investor can passively earn **12%–15% CAGR** in the Nifty 50 index, buying an individual small-cap or mid-cap stock requires a **minimum 15% risk-adjusted return** in the **Base Case** [[01:37:19–01:38:47](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5839)]:

- If the **Base Case CAGR is \> 15%** at the current market price (without relying on Bull Case P/E re-rating), **Margin of Safety already exists** in today's stock price [[01:37:19–01:38:47](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5839)].  
- If the **Base Case CAGR is \< 15%** (e.g., 9% CAGR in RACL Geartech at ₹1,323 crore market cap), the investor must wait for a price drawdown (or time correction) until the entry market cap yields at least **15%–16% CAGR in the Base Case** [[01:15:11–01:16:56](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4511)].

#### Using Credit Rating Reports & Capital Employed Mix to Spot Re-Rating Narratives

Credit rating rationale reports (such as CRISIL) provide institutional-grade disclosures on segment-wise volume trends, management revenue growth guidance (often within 1–2 lines), debt-funded Capex plans, and global market share [[01:18:20–01:23:39](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4700)]. ### a) What this stage answers

Why are interest rates like gravity to stock valuations, how does a lower foreign institutional (FII) discount rate (6% vs. 10%) inflate intrinsic values, and how do you execute a complete 10-year Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Terminal Value calculation?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Interest Rates as Gravity & The Market as a Discounting Machine

Interest rates act like **gravity on stock valuations**: the higher the interest rates, the lower the valuations; the lower the interest rates, the higher the valuations (if interest rates were zero, valuations could approach infinity)  [[01:48:44–01:49:38](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6524), [02:10:01–02:10:49](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7801)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript]. Even if an investor does not use formal DCF spreadsheets for day-to-day stock picking, understanding DCF is mandatory because **the stock market is a forward discounting machine** [[01:49:38–01:52:38](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6578)]:

- **Why Cash Today Beats Cash Tomorrow:** People prefer present consumption over future consumption; inflation erodes purchasing power over time (e.g., hyperinflation in the Turkish lira from 30 lira to 3,000 lira forces immediate spending); and promised future cash flows carry waiting risk [[01:52:38–01:54:28](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6758)].  
- **John Burr Williams & Aesop's "Bird in the Hand" (Buffett's 1992 Letter):** Quoting Warren Buffett's 1992 shareholder letter, John Burr Williams (*The Theory of Investment Value*) defined the value of any stock, bond, or business as its expected remaining cash inflows and outflows discounted at an appropriate interest rate—a formula unchanged since **Aesop in 600 BC** (*"A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush"*), which depends on answering three questions [[01:54:28–01:56:33](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6868)]:  
  1. How certain are you that there are indeed birds in the bush?  
  2. When will they emerge, and how many will there be?  
  3. What is the risk-free interest rate?

#### Compounding vs. Discounting & The Three Pillars of DCF

**Compounding is multiplication (about tomorrow), whereas Discounting is division (about today)** [[01:59:48–02:04:29](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7188)]:

$
\Compounding:  \FV \= \PV \× (1 \+ r)^n \\quad \\Longleftrightarrow \\quad \Discounting:  \PV \= \\frac{\FV}{(1 \+ r)^n}
$

(For example, ₹100 compounded at 10% for 2 years \= $100 \times 1.1 \times 1.1 = \mathbf{\text{₹}121}$; discounting ₹121 back 2 years at 10% \= $121 \div (1.1)^2 = \mathbf{\text{₹}100}$).

Every DCF rests on **three inputs**  [[02:04:29–02:05:00](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7469), [02:14:25–02:16:47](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8065)]:

1. **Growth in Cash Flows (Owner's Earnings / Free Cash Flow):** $\text{FCF} = \text{Cash Flow from Operations (CFO)} - \text{Maintenance/Replacement Capex}$ (approximated by annual Depreciation) [[02:17:49–02:20:06](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8269)].  
2. **Discount Rate ($r$):** Opportunity cost of capital (\~10%–12% in India vs. \~6% for foreign institutional investors / FIIs whose home-market cost of capital is 1%–3%). Dividing ₹121 by $(1.06)^2$ instead of $(1.10)^2$ raises present value from **₹100 to ₹107.69**—explaining why rising FII ownership re-rates mid-to-large-cap compounders to seemingly expensive P/Es [[02:05:00–02:14:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7500)].  
3. **Terminal Value (Post-Year 10):** Because no business can grow at 10%–15% for 200 years when world GDP grows 3% and India's real GDP moderates toward 4%–6%, assume a perpetual **Terminal Growth Rate ($g$) of 2%–4%** (4% baseline) after Year 10, compute Terminal Value at Year 10 via the Gordon Growth formula, and discount it back to Year 0  [[02:15:16–02:15:53](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8116), [02:29:11–02:37:31](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8951)]:

$
\Terminal Year CF*{11} \= \CF*{10} \× (1 \+ g), \\quad \Terminal Value*{10} \= \\frac{\Terminal Year CF*{11}}{r \- g}, \\quad \PV of Terminal Value \= \\frac{\Terminal Value\_{10}}{(1 \+ r)^{10}}
$

## CC-Lec07 – Stage 5: DCF Mindset, Reverse DCF & Market-Share / Terminal Risks [[02:41:22–02:54:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9682)] [TVGP: Valuation]

### a) What this stage answers

Why does Terminal Value (\~60% of total DCF intrinsic value) govern whether a business gets a capped 20x–25x P/E or a high institutional multiple, and how do you use Reverse DCF to calculate the implied 5-year earnings growth baked into an expensive stock price?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Terminal Value (\~60% of Intrinsic Value) & Disruption vs. Longevity

In a 10-year Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) calculation, **Terminal Value accounts for roughly 60% of a stock's total intrinsic valuation** (as demonstrated in Stage 4, where ₹46,380 Cr of Varun Beverages' ₹66,388 Cr DCF value came from Terminal Value) [[02:41:22–02:42:02](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9682)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript]. Because the bulk of present value sits beyond Year 10, the market assigns high P/E multiples only when investors have strong conviction in a company's multi-decade longevity and terminal cash flows:

- **Terminal Value Disruption Cap:** When an industry faces structural disruption over the next 20–30 years (such as internal combustion engine auto-exhaust manufacturers facing electric vehicle adoption), investors discount its terminal value heavily and cap its P/E multiple at **20x–25x** [[02:42:02–02:42:52](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9722)]. However, under the DCF mindset, if the market assumes only 5% cash flow growth for such a company and actual near-term cash flow growth accelerates to 25% for 1–2 years, its intrinsic value and stock price will still rise meaningfully even without a P/E expansion beyond 20x–25x [[02:42:52–02:43:42](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9772)].  
- **National vs. Regional Retail Multiples & Embedded DCF Expectations:** In consumer/retail sectors, national brands with long reinvestment runways and foreign institutional (FII) sponsorship command much higher multiples than regional players [[02:43:42–02:45:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9822)]. When a younger national/multi-regional peer demonstrates sustainable cash flow and profitability growth alongside rising FII ownership, the gap between its 34x P/E and peers at 64x–97x P/E creates a re-rating opportunity (Note: in jewellery retail, operating cash flow is often negative during store expansion because working capital is tied up in gold inventory, requiring investors to evaluate inventory turns and concall disclosures rather than raw CFO alone)  [[02:44:19–02:45:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9859), [02:52:05–02:52:49](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10325)]. Conversely, when an Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) company trades at **125x P/E**, the market's implicit DCF is pricing in explosive (\~80%) growth; if actual earnings growth comes in at a healthy 20%–30% instead of 80%, the stock suffers a sharp P/E de-rating [[02:45:10–02:45:56](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9910)].

#### Reverse DCF (Expectations Investing) & Portfolio Risk Sizing

While a standard DCF requires assuming future cash flow growth rates to estimate fair value, **Reverse DCF** flips the equation: it takes the **current market price / market cap**, **current TTM earnings (PAT)** (used instead of CFO when working-capital intensity makes CFO negative), **discount rate (required return)**, and an **assumed exit P/E multiple** after 5 years to compute the **exact 5-year earnings CAGR already embedded in the stock price** [[02:46:42–02:50:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10002)].

- **Why Manual Reverse DCF Is Difficult:** Computing Reverse DCF manually in Excel requires using the `Goal Seek` function, which is cumbersome; investors can instead use automated Reverse DCF calculators such as **Tijori Finance** (using coupon code `SOIC025` for a 10% discount) or SOIC's upcoming free Stock Scan Reverse DCF tool [[02:46:42–02:49:36](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10002)].  
- **Combating Linear Extrapolation & Sizing Risk:** Human beings habitually extrapolate 1–2 years of booming earnings linearly into the next 5 years, bidding hyper-optimistic stocks and SME/microcap themes to unsustainable multiples [[02:51:25–02:52:05](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10285)]. Investors who witnessed the 2018 cycle saw microcap stocks crash **90%–95%** when growth slowed [[02:52:05–02:52:49](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10325)]. Therefore:  
  1. Lower starting multiples offer higher statistical odds of wealth creation, while high-multiple stocks require strict **technical analysis exit frameworks** (covered in Stage 6\) [[02:52:49–02:53:35](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10369)].  
  2. Speaker's rule: Never allocate more than **10%–12% of total portfolio capital** combined across SME / frothy high-multiple bets (3–4 SME names max), because when growth falters in small companies, they blow up severely [[02:53:35–02:54:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10415)].

### a) What this stage answers

How do you value a deep-cyclical commodity stock at trough margins using post-Capex Gross Block, Asset Turnover, Peak EBITDA Margins, Peak EV/EBITDA, and Price-to-Sales (P/S), and how do you time the entry with the 30-week EMA?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Cyclical Commodity Valuation Framework (Trough-to-Peak Math)

Unlike secular compounders (or wealth-management businesses like Anand Rathi that are currently parabolic), cyclical commodity manufacturers experience sharp swings in product realizations and operating margins [[03:20:10–03:22:42](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12010)] [PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript]:

- **Why P/E Fails at the Bottom of a Commodity Cycle:** At the trough of a commodity cycle, operating margins sit at 10-year lows and net profits vanish or turn into losses, making the trailing P/E ratio look absurdly high (e.g., **1,800x P/E**) or NA [[03:21:46](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12106)].  
- **Four-Step Trough-to-Peak Cyclical Valuation Method:**  
  1. **Verify Trough Margins:** Check 10-year historical Operating Profit Margins (OPM) to confirm that current margins are at the bottom of the 10-year range due to depressed commodity prices [[03:20:56–03:21:46](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12056)].  
  2. **Project Post-Capex Peak Revenue via Asset Turnover:** Take the company's expanded **Gross Block (Fixed Assets)** post-Capex and multiply it by its normal **Asset Turnover ratio** ($\text{Current Sales} \div \text{Current Fixed Assets}$) to estimate full-utilization revenue [[03:22:42–03:23:28](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12162)].  
  3. **Estimate Peak Cyclical EBITDA:** Apply the company's historical up-cycle **Operating Margin (e.g., 20%)** to projected full-utilization sales to calculate **Peak EBITDA** [[03:22:42–03:23:28](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12162)].  
  4. **Apply Peak EV/EBITDA & Cross-Check with P/B and P/S:** Multiply Peak EBITDA by historical peak **EV/EBITDA multiples (7x to 10x)**, and cross-verify using **5-year Price-to-Book (P/B)** and **10-year Price-to-Sales (P/S)** (checking that current P/S is near its 10-year low and projecting peak Market Cap at **2x P/S**) [[03:23:28–03:24:52](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12208)].  
- **Timing the Cyclical Entry (30-Week EMA \+ Credit Rating Report):** Because trough losses and commissioning delays can drag on, do not buy blindly during the downturn. Read the **credit rating report** to track Capex commissioning and margin expansion drivers, and wait for the stock to **cross above its 30-week EMA with very high volume**—confirming that informed buyers are pricing in a rebound in commodity realizations [[03:24:14–03:25:38](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12254)].  
- **Never Confuse Cyclicals with Secular Compounders:** Comparing a cyclical commodity stock (like Thirumalai Chemicals or NALCO, which must be traded across 2–3 cycles per decade) with a secular compounder (like SRF held for 10 years) is fundamentally flawed; each requires its own valuation and exit playbook [[03:25:38–03:27:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12338)].

## 7\. GLOSSARY

- **2-4-6-8% Strategy (4-6-8-10% Framework):** Portfolio position-sizing rule capping total holdings at 20–25 stocks with minimum 2%–4% starter weights, 6% core weights, and 8%–10% maximum conviction weights [[03:16:38–03:17:08](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=11798)].  
- **30-Week EMA (Exponential Moving Average):** Core weekly trend filter in Stan Weinstein / Wyckoff stage analysis separating Stage 2 markup (above rising 30-W EMA) from Stage 4 markdown (below declining 30-W EMA) [[02:56:53–02:58:45](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10613)].  
- **ADX (Average Directional Index):** Indicator component inside the TradingView SOIC LTI tool that can be set to zero when checking pure monthly V-Stop signals [[03:19:16–03:20:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=11956)].  
- **Alpha:** Excess portfolio or stock return generated above the benchmark index (such as Nifty 50\) over a given period [[00:18:20–00:22:34](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1100)].  
- **Aluminium Formwork:** Reusable construction casting system replacing traditional wood/steel scaffolding in real estate and infrastructure projects, driving high-ROCE growth for Technocraft Industries  [[01:21:38–01:23:39](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4898), [01:27:40–01:29:31](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5260)].  
- **Asset Turnover (Fixed Asset Turn):** Ratio of annual revenue to fixed assets / gross block ($\text{Sales} \div \text{Fixed Assets}$), used to project incremental revenue from planned Capex  [[01:03:21–01:04:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3801), [03:22:42–03:23:28](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12162)].  
- **BAP (Buy at Any Price):** Investing philosophy popularized around 2018–2019 of buying high-quality companies at 50x–100x P/E regardless of starting valuation [[00:03:34–00:06:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=214)].  
- **Bull, Base, and Bear Scenarios:** Three-tier forward P\&L and exit-multiple forecast modeling optimistic (Bull), realistic/conservative without re-rating (Base), and downside/recessionary (Bear) outcomes over 3–4 years [[00:59:24–01:15:11](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3564)].  
- **Capital Cycle:** Self-correcting economic cycle wherein high returns attract capital inflows (QIPs, IPOs, new plants) until excess competition drives returns below the cost of capital, followed by capital flight (Capex/IPO deferrals) and consolidation that restore high returns [[00:27:34–00:32:26](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1654)].  
- **CAPM / Beta:** Capital Asset Pricing Model and volatility metric discussed in academic valuation texts; speaker notes investors can skip them in practical valuation [[00:59:02–00:59:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3542)].  
- **CDMO:** Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization (Used without definition) [[00:14:41](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=881)].  
- **Compounding vs. Discounting:** Compounding multiplies present value to find future value ($\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1+r)^n$, about tomorrow); discounting divides future cash flows to find present value ($\text{PV} = \text{FV} \div (1+r)^n$, about today) [[01:59:48–02:04:29](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7188)].  
- **Credit Rating Report (CRISIL):** Institutional credit assessment document disclosing segment revenue drivers, management growth guidance, Capex plans, and working-capital debt [[01:18:20–01:23:39](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4700)].  
- **DCF (Discounted Cash Flow):** Valuation method calculating intrinsic value as the sum of discounted future Free Cash Flows over an explicit forecast period (e.g., 10 years) plus the discounted Terminal Value [[01:49:38–02:39:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6578)].  
- **DII Investment Committee:** Institutional screening body inside domestic mutual funds (AMCs) that vets corporate governance and capital allocation before approving stock purchases [[00:53:59–00:58:30](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=3239)].  
- **Discount Rate ($r$):** Opportunity cost of capital or required rate of return used to discount future cash flows back to present value (\~10%–12% in India vs. \~6% for FIIs) [[02:04:29–02:14:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7469)].  
- **Drum Closures:** Industrial sealing caps/clamps for steel and plastic barrels where Technocraft Industries holds a 36% global market share ex-China [[01:19:48–01:20:19](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4788)].  
- **EMS (Electronic Manufacturing Services):** Contract electronics assembly sector experiencing heavy QIP, mainboard/SME IPO, and private plant inflows at 125x–150x P/E  [[00:26:52](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1612), [00:30:22–00:32:26](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1822), [02:45:10–02:51:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=9910)].  
- **FII (Foreign Institutional Investor):** Overseas institutional capital with a low home-market cost of capital (1%–3%) that applies a \~6% discount rate in India, driving up valuations of low-float compounders [[02:06:03–02:14:25](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=7563)].  
- **Goal Seek:** Excel function required to solve manually for implied growth rates in a Reverse DCF [[02:48:48–02:49:36](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10128)].  
- **Gross Block:** Total historical acquisition cost of fixed assets/plant and machinery before depreciation, used with asset turnover to estimate peak cyclical revenue [[03:22:42–03:23:28](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12162)].  
- **Intrinsic Value P/E vs. Transactional P/E:** Intrinsic P/E reflects the business's underlying cash-flow generating power and growth; Transactional P/E reflects current market sentiment, emotion, and institutional buying/selling pressure [[00:42:10–00:46:44](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2530)].  
- **Late-Stage Base:** A consolidation base numbered 3, 4, 5 or higher within a prolonged Stage 2 advance, carrying elevated failure risk as institutional ownership crowds and margins peak  [[03:06:49–03:08:12](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=11209), [03:17:08–03:18:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=11828)].

- **Margin of Safety (MoS):** Downside buffer present when a stock's conservative Base Case expected return exceeds 15% CAGR (the Nifty 50 opportunity cost) without assuming P/E expansion  [[01:15:11–01:16:56](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=4511), [01:37:19–01:38:47](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5839)].  
- **MBED (Market-Based Economic Dispatch):** Proposed regulatory power-market reform and market coupling mechanism that triggered de-rating fears in IEX [[00:43:23–00:44:21](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=2603)].  
- **Owner's Earnings (Free Cash Flow / FCF):** Operating cash flow (CFO) minus maintenance/replacement Capex (Depreciation), representing cash available for shareholders [[02:17:49–02:20:06](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8269)].  
- **Parabolic Blow-Off:** Vertical price surge accompanied by lifetime-high volume bars at the end of a Stage 2 advance, signaling smart-money distribution and a Stage 3 top [[02:58:15–02:59:24](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10695)].  
- **PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth):** Trailing 12-month P/E divided by expected 1-year forward earnings growth percentage [[00:16:49–00:18:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=1009)].

- **RATE Function:** Excel financial formula `RATE(nper, pmt, pv, fv)` used to calculate compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between negative current Market Cap (`pv`) and projected future Market Cap (`fv`)  [[01:32:40–01:33:51](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=5560), [01:44:20](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=6260)].

- **SOIC LTI Tool:** Proprietary TradingView technical indicator combining 30-week EMA, Weekly/Monthly V-Stop (1.5x–2.0x multiplier), and ADX for Stage Analysis entry and exit triggers  [[02:58:45–03:02:41](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=10725), [03:19:16–03:20:10](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=11956), [03:30:04](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=12604)].  
- **Terminal Value:** Estimated present value at Year 10 of all future cash flows beyond the explicit 10-year forecast period, growing perpetually at 2%–4% ($g$) and discounted back to today  [[02:15:16–02:15:53](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8116), [02:29:11–02:37:31](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec07?t=8951)].

## 11\. SELF-TEST

1. **Conceptual (Stage 1):** Why did the FY19 21-stock BAP ("Buy at Any Price") portfolio of high-quality companies underperform the Nifty 50 over 2018–2023 (94% vs. 97.8%), and what are the speaker's hard PEG and starting P/E thresholds from the 2018 Wealth Creation Study?  
   - **Answer:** Buying a basket at \>50x–60x P/E (\~3x the Nifty 50's 20x P/E) leaves zero room for multiple expansion and triggers P/E de-rating whenever growth moderates. The hard rule is **PEG ≤ 1.5x** (stretching up to **≤ 2.0x** strictly for predictable consumer stocks like Varun Beverages at 1.67x) and a **starting P/E sweet spot of 30x–40x** (as P/E ≥ 50x–75x+ generates negative 3-year alpha).  
2. **Conceptual (Stage 2):** How does the Capital Cycle work across expansion and contraction phases, and what real-world corporate actions signal each phase?  
   - **Answer:** In the expansion phase, high sector returns attract optimism, waves of **QIPs**, **mainboard/SME IPOs**, and unlisted capacity additions (e.g., EMS, Railways, Wires & Cables, and 20–25 new Noida EMS plants), eventually driving returns below the cost of capital. In the contraction phase, pessimism causes **IPO and Capex deferrals** (e.g., Balaji Speciality Chemicals IPO deferral and Gujarat Fluorochemicals Capex deferral), consolidating supply until returns exceed the cost of capital.  
3. **Conceptual (Stage 2):** Differentiate between Intrinsic Value P/E and Transactional P/E using the examples of PSU Banks, Delta Corp, and Balaji Amines vs. Alkyl Amines.  
   - **Answer:** Intrinsic P/E reflects cash-flow and earnings power, whereas Transactional P/E reflects sentiment and institutional sponsorship. In PSU Banks, Intrinsic P/E recovered via high ROE and low GNPA, but Transactional P/E remains depressed by past memory; in Delta Corp, 28% GST impaired both; and in Balaji Amines (37x P/E vs. Alkyl Amines at 68x P/E), opening an unrelated hotel caused DII investment committees to veto the stock, capping its Transactional P/E.  
4. **Applied Calculation (Stage 3):** RACL Geartech has base sales of ₹358 Cr, PAT of ₹37 Cr, Market Cap of ₹1,323 Cr, and plans ₹250 Cr Capex over 5 years (\~₹50 Cr/year) at 2x asset turnover. Using the speaker's 10-year depreciation shortcut, how much incremental depreciation is added per year, why did the speaker pass on buying at ₹1,323 Cr Market Cap, and what Market Cap range provides Margin of Safety?  
   - **Answer:** ₹50 Cr annual Capex $\div$ 10-year life adds **\+₹5 Cr incremental depreciation per year**. At ₹1,323 Cr Market Cap, the Base Scenario (20% growth post-FY24, ₹69.37 Cr FY27 PAT, 25x exit P/E \= ₹1,734 Cr Market Cap) yields only **9% CAGR**, below the **\>15% Base Case Margin of Safety hurdle**. Waiting for a 15%–18% correction to **₹1,000–1,100 Cr Market Cap** lifts the Base Case return to **16% CAGR**.  
5. **Applied Calculation (Stage 3):** In Technocraft Industries (FY23 Sales \= ₹1,985 Cr, Base Depreciation \= ₹64 Cr, Market Cap \= ₹5,292 Cr), how does the ₹350 Cr scaffolding/aluminium formwork Capex transform its Capital Employed mix, and why does the current price offer Margin of Safety?  
   - **Answer:** Directing ₹350 Cr Capex entirely into high-ROCE scaffolding/formwork expands total Capital Employed from \~₹1,700–1,800 Cr to \~₹2,200 Cr, shrinking low-ROCE textile/power capital from **25% (\~₹400 Cr) down to 17%** and lifting value-added businesses to **82%**. Keeping depreciation at **3.22% of sales** ($64 \div 1,985$) and adding 9% interest on incremental debt, the **Base Case (18x–20x exit P/E, no re-rating) delivers 24% CAGR** (\>15% hurdle), while a Bull Case re-rating to 30x P/E yields **52% CAGR**.  
6. **Applied Calculation (Stage 4):** Walk through Varun Beverages' 10-year DCF calculation starting from ₹2,000 Cr CFO and ₹700 Cr Maintenance Capex/Depreciation, given Year-10 FCF of ₹6,938 Cr, $r = 10%$, and $g = 4%$. Year-11 terminal cash flow \= $\text{₹}6,938 \times 1.04 = \mathbf{\text{₹}7,216\text{ Cr}}$; Year-10 Terminal Value \= $\text{₹}7,216 \div (0.10 - 0.04) = \mathbf{\text{₹}1,20,266\text{ Cr}}$; discounted by $1.1^{10}$, PV of Terminal Value $\approx \mathbf{\text{₹}46,380\text{ Cr}}$. Total Intrinsic Market Cap at 10% $r$ \= $\text{₹}20,008 + \text{₹}46,380 = \mathbf{\text{₹}66,388\text{ Cr}}$ (**₹510/share** vs. ₹1,070 CMP). At a **6% FII discount rate**, Intrinsic Market Cap jumps to **₹2,26,808 Cr** (**₹1,744/share**).  
7. **Applied Calculation (Stage 5):** In the Tijori Finance Reverse DCF for Kaynes Technology (TTM PAT \= ₹121 Cr, current P/E \= 125x, assumed Year-5 exit P/E \= 30x–40x), what 5-year PAT CAGRs are required to earn 10%, 15%, 20%, and 25% annual returns, and what portfolio sizing rule applies to such bets?

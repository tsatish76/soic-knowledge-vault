### 2\. KEY LEARNINGS

- **Buffett's Key-Variables & Real-Estate Appraisal Rule:** Effective valuation does not require 5-decimal-place precision; it requires identifying the 4 to 5 critical variables driving a business's future cash flows and staying strictly within your circle of competence by avoiding unpredictable businesses \[[00:09:00–00:12:39](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=540), [02:39:56–02:41:45\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9596)].  
- **Odds & The Hard Ceiling on High P/E Multiples:** Paying 70x–100x+ P/E stacks the odds heavily against the investor (like betting ₹1 lakh on Manchester City to win ₹1.3 lakh vs. asymmetric underdogs) because peak margins and peak earnings are already priced in and P/E cannot expand infinitely \[[00:13:16–00:24:36\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=796)].  
- **Alkyl Amines Entry vs. Peak Risk-Reward:** Entering when Capex (₹300–₹400 Cr) drives future sales (₹846 Cr → ₹1,542 Cr) at **0.6x PEG** (paying 60 paise for ₹1 of growth) delivers asymmetric upside, whereas buying at **70x–75x P/E** on peak 42% margins (vs. 16%–20% historical average) invites multi-year de-rating \[[00:22:29–00:36:42\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1349)].  
- **Five Uses of Valuation & The Overhang of Overpaying:** Valuation identifies sectors to avoid, peak margins \+ peak danger, outperforming sectors, business growth phases, and emerging value in beaten-down sectors \[[00:38:31–00:41:53\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2311)]. Overpaying (Clean Science 120x, IEX 110x, IRCTC 100x, Dr. Lal 95x, DMart 230x, Relaxo 100x, BKT Tyres 40x+) creates a multi-year "hangover" where even 10%–11% earnings growth yields zero returns \[[00:50:16–00:52:19](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=3016), [01:08:30–01:14:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4110)].  
- **P/E Re-Rating vs. De-Rating (Haunted House to Airport Analogy):** A business with stagnant/negative perception trades at depressed multiples ("haunted house" de-rating), whereas an external catalyst (airport/metro connectivity, replacement cycle, or industry tailwind) triggers rapid multiple expansion ("re-rating") \[[00:57:58–01:05:18\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=3478)].  
- **P/E as Perception-to-Earnings & Intrinsic vs. Transactional Value:** Earnings Yield (\$\\frac{1}{\\text{P/E}} \\times 100\$) benchmarks stock returns against real estate/bonds \[[01:18:36–01:21:00\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4716)]. Assets without cash flows (paintings, Bitcoin, Solana) trade purely on **transactional value**, whereas stocks trade on **intrinsic value** (future cash flows \+ governance) multiplied by **perception** \[[01:23:02–01:29:56\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4982)].  
- **Governance & Cash-Flow Traps at Low P/E:** A low 10x P/E stock with shady corporate governance, missing cash flows (e.g., profits trapped in receivables), or high-volume Stage 4 breakdowns (\>8-year volume spikes) is a value trap to avoid at all costs \[[01:33:41–01:39:45\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5621)].  
- **Infosys (2000 vs. 2002\) & PEG \< 1.5–2x Rule:** Even when Infosys grew PAT at 100%+ CAGR, buying at **206x P/E** in 2000 produced 5 years of zero returns, whereas buying at **30x P/E** in 2002 compounded wealth; target **PEG \< 1.5x–2.0x** \[[02:11:37–02:16:11\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=7897)].  
- **Where Static P/E Fails:** Trailing P/E misleads in (1) Financials/Life Insurance (use Price-to-Embedded Value / Price-to-Book), (2) deep cyclicals, (3) cornered-float stocks (Divgi TorqTransfer Systems, \~90% float locked), (4) one-off windfalls (Divi's Labs COVID Molnupiravir, Natco Pharma first-to-file Revlimid), and (5) pre-commissioning Capex (Deepak Nitrite at 81x P/E before its phenol plant → 11x) \[[02:17:31–02:27:57](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8251), [02:37:09–02:39:56\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9429)].  
- **2-Year Forward P/E Discipline:** Estimate earnings at most **2 years forward** (keeping current market cap static: \$\\text{Forward P/E} \= \\text{Current Mcap} \\div \\text{Year-2 PAT}\$) to verify whether a seemingly expensive stock is actually cheap on upcoming Capex/utilization \[[02:27:57–02:36:37\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8877)].

## CC-Lec05 – Stage 2: Buffett & Munger Video \+ Samir Arora Interview: Key Variables, Odds & Limits to High P/E \[[00:07:48–00:26:37\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=468)] \[TVGP: Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

What do Warren Buffett, Charlie Munger, and Samir Arora teach about identifying key business variables, playing favorable odds, and respecting the upper limits of P/E multiples?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Video 1: Warren Buffett & Charlie Munger on Teaching Valuation

In a Berkshire Hathaway Q\&A clip (asked by Columbia Business School PhD student James Claus), Warren Buffett explains how Ben Graham taught valuation through comparative case studies—sometimes giving Company A and Company B figures that turned out to be the same company at different points in history, or administering a 1968 San Diego true/false test that everyone flunked \[[00:07:48–00:10:25\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=468)]. Buffett's core teaching rules \[[00:10:25–00:12:39\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=625)]:

1. **Identify Key Variables & Predictability:** Study one business after another to isolate the few dominant variables driving that business and evaluate how predictable they are; **if a business is not predictable, forget it**.  
2. **No 5-Decimal Precision Needed:** You do not need to value a company to five decimal places; you only need to know in a general way that you are right on the dominant variables ("fat pitch" — a dozen big decisions in a lifetime).  
3. **Avoid False Precision:** Valuation cannot be taught like real estate appraising for every company. Charlie Munger adds that on a final exam he would ask students to value an internet company and **flunk anyone who gave an answer** \[[00:11:46–00:12:39\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=706)].

#### Video 2: Samir Arora on High P/E Multiples & "Playing the Odds"

In a Moneycontrol interview with Mahalakshmi, fund manager Samir Arora addresses the post-COVID narrative that market leaders at 80x–100x P/E deserve infinite multiples for pricing power \[[00:12:39–00:17:44\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=759)]:

- **India's Price Sensitivity:** Consumers rarely accept arbitrary price hikes (e.g., Facebook's \$1/year subscription failing in India; Dish TV in 2014–2016 failing to push ARPU to ₹250–₹300; PVR being a rare exception).  
- **Multiple Creep for Same Growth:** If a company historically grew at 20% and traded at 30x–40x P/E, paying **70x P/E for the exact same 20% growth** makes no sense.  
- **Playing the Odds:** Multiples cannot expand infinitely to 100x–200x; there is a ceiling. Even great businesses like DMart underperformed the broader market after hitting extreme valuations \[[00:16:20–00:17:44\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=980)].

#### The Betting Odds Analogy & Situation A vs. Situation B

In investing, as in sports betting, **odds determine non-linear payoffs** \[[00:20:10–00:22:29\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1210)]. Buying a high-P/E stock where peak earnings and peak margins are already discovered is like betting on a heavy favorite for tiny upside and massive downside risk \[[00:22:29–00:26:37\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1349)].

## CC-Lec05 – Stage 3: Alkyl Amines Case Study (0.6x PEG to 75x P/E), Risk-Reward & Why Valuations Matter \[[00:26:37–00:42:27\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1597)] \[TVGP: Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

How do you evaluate a business using just 3–4 key variables and the PEG ratio, how does the Entry-vs.-Exit return equation expose risk-reward, and what are the five core reasons valuations matter?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Mean Reversion, Business Cycles & Simple PEG Valuation

Industries cycle through 3–4 year doublings followed by 3–4 year slowdowns (recommended book: *Capital*, \~100–150 pages on capitalism's bull/bear/bust cycles) \[[00:27:12–00:30:44\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1632)]. Whenever margins jump far above their 10–11 year historical average, question sustainability because **margins revert to the mean** \[[00:27:12–00:29:26\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1632)].

Applying Buffett's key-variables approach \+ Arora's odds \[[00:30:44–00:34:21\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1844)]:

- Focus on 3–4 dominant variables (industry structure, Capex, fixed-asset turns) rather than complex DCF jugglery.  
- Compute the **PEG Ratio**: \$\$\\text{PEG Ratio} \= \\frac{\\text{P/E Ratio}}{\\text{Expected Earnings Growth (%)}}\$\$ Buying at **0.6x PEG** means paying **60 paise for ₹1 of growth**—stacking odds heavily in your favor \[[00:32:00–00:33:44\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1920)].

#### The Entry vs. Exit Risk-Reward Equation

Stock returns are governed by \[[00:35:01–00:37:28\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2101)]: \$\$\\text{Entry Price} \= \\text{Entry P/E} \\times \\text{Entry Earnings} \\quad \\longrightarrow \\quad \\text{Exit Price} \= \\text{Exit P/E} \\times \\text{Exit Earnings}\$\$ For most businesses, paying **40x–45x P/E** becomes risky (aside from rare consumer exceptions) \[[00:35:45\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2145)]. Buying at **75x P/E** on peak earnings bets simultaneously that earnings will grow 3–4x in 2–3 years AND that the 75x P/E will sustain—if the cycle turns, **both earnings and P/E collapse together** \[[00:35:45–00:37:28\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2145)].

#### Five Reasons Why Valuations Matter

1. **Identifies Sectors to Avoid:** 100x–140x P/E signals sector euphoria/froth (requiring a momentum mindset, not a valuation mindset) \[[00:38:31–00:39:18\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2311)].  
2. **Flags Peak Margins \+ Peak Danger:** Alerts you before mean reversion hits \[[00:39:18\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2358)].  
3. **Reveals Outperforming Sectors:** Sectors with strong earnings growth (e.g., Auto & Auto Ancillary) often trade at reasonable multiples relative to growth, offering re-rating odds \[[00:39:18–00:40:03\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2358)].  
4. **Clarifies Business Growth Phases:** Maps where a company sits in its expansion cycle \[[00:40:03–00:40:41\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2403)].  
5. **Uncovers Emerging Value:** Keeps you open-minded to beaten-down sectors where value may emerge \[[00:40:41–00:41:15\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=2441)].

## CC-Lec05 – Stage 6: What is P/E Ratio? Earnings Yield, Intrinsic vs. Transactional Value & Perception-to-Earnings \[[01:17:18–01:33:41\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4638)] \[TVGP: Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

What does the P/E ratio mean from first principles (and as Earnings Yield), and how do Intrinsic Value and Transactional Value combine to form "Perception-to-Earnings"?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### P/E Ratio & Earnings Yield from First Principles

The **P/E Ratio** measures how many rupees you are willing to pay for ₹1 of an asset's annual earnings \[[01:18:04–01:21:40\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4684)] \[PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript\]: \$\$\\text{P/E Ratio} \= \\frac{\\text{Market Cap}}{\\text{Net Profit (TTM)}} \= \\frac{\\text{Stock Price}}{\\text{EPS}}\$\$ Inverting P/E gives **Earnings Yield** (\$\\frac{\\text{Earnings}}{\\text{Price}} \\times 100\$), allowing direct comparison with real estate rental yields or fixed deposits (10x P/E \= 10% yield; 20x P/E \= 5% yield) \[[01:19:25–01:21:00\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4765)].

#### Intrinsic Value vs. Transactional Value ("Perception-to-Earnings")

Every P/E multiple is driven by two forces \[[01:23:02–01:31:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4982)]:

1. **Transactional Value (Perception / Sentiment):** What other market participants are willing to pay for the asset (e.g., the Mona Lisa painting, Bitcoin, or Solana—none of which pay cash flows to an individual holder). Technical analysis operates on transactional value \[[01:24:29–01:28:33\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5069)].  
2. **Intrinsic Value (Cash Flows & Governance):** The actual cash flows and dividends a business generates, its earnings growth rate, and management integrity/ethics \[[01:24:29–01:25:22](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5069), [01:28:33–01:29:56\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5313)].

Speaker's view: Rather than "Price-to-Earnings," P/E should be understood as **Perception-to-Earnings Ratio**—where \$\\text{Stock Value} \= \\text{Intrinsic Value (Earnings)} \\times \\text{Transactional Value (Perception)}\$ \[[01:29:06–01:31:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5346)]. Sectoral fancy and sudden earnings acceleration (e.g., 10% → 40% growth) rapidly improve perception and re-rate the multiple \[[01:29:56–01:31:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5396)].

## CC-Lec05 – Stage 8: Infosys (2000–2002) Case Study: Starting Valuations, PEG \< 1.5–2x & Where P/E Fails \[[02:08:59–02:27:57\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=7739)] \[TVGP: Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Recommended Books & The Infosys (2000–2002) Lesson

Before diving into Infosys, three books are highlighted \[[02:09:44–02:10:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=7784)]: *Capital Returns* by Edward Chancellor (Marathon Asset Management), *The Biggest Bluff* ("biggest book" / "Biggest glove" \[?\]) on probability, and *The Intelligence Trap* by David Robson.

Using Ace Equity data for **Infosys** during the 2000 IT mania: even when PAT grew 100%–117%, buying at **206x P/E** in 2000 produced **5 years of negative returns (-5% CAGR)** once growth normalized to 30% \[[02:11:37–02:14:45\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=7897)]. As Prashant Jain repeatedly emphasized: *"Good businesses at wrong prices don't make for good investments"* \[[02:16:11\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8171)]. In every market cycle (IT at 300x P/E in 2000; DLF/Real Estate in 2007), markets invent a "new normal" narrative—so target **PEG \< 1.5x to 2.0x** (and **PEG \< 1.0x** when the market expects zero growth but earnings surprise upward) \[[02:15:31–02:17:31\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8131)].

#### Five Cases Where Trailing P/E Fails

1. **Banks, Real Estate & Life Insurance:** Require sector-specific multiples—**Price-to-Book** (banks), **Pre-sales / Project Cash Flows** (real estate), and **Price-to-Embedded Value** (life insurance) (covered in Part 2\) \[[02:17:31–02:18:23\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8251)].  
2. **Deep Cyclicals & Loss-Making Companies:** Trailing P/E looks absurdly high at cycle troughs (e.g., 80x P/E when quarterly PAT is ₹1 crore, collapsing to 30x P/E once quarterly PAT rebounds to ₹40 crore) \[[02:18:23–02:18:39\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8303)].  
3. **Cornered-Float Stocks:** When 90%+ of shares are locked with promoters/institutions, tiny daily volumes keep P/E artificially elevated despite weak quarters—until large holders sell \[[02:18:39–02:22:00\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8319)].  
4. **One-Off Earnings:** Exceptional gains (like contract termination fees) artificially depress trailing P/E, tricking retail investors into thinking an expensive stock is cheap \[[02:22:00–02:25:01\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8520)].  
5. **Operating Leverage / Upcoming Capex:** Heavy Capex ahead of commercialization makes trailing P/E look 80x–100x expensive right before earnings explode \[[02:25:01–02:27:57\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8701)].

## CC-Lec05 – Stage 9: Forward P/E Mechanics, First-to-File One-Offs (Natco Pharma) & Key Variables \[[02:27:57–02:45:40\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8877)] \[TVGP: Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

How do you calculate 2-year Forward P/E while keeping market cap static, why do first-to-file pharma windfalls fail to re-rate, and how do "Variables → Valuation → Technicals" work together?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Two-Year Forward P/E Mechanics & Buffett's Mental DCF

When Capex or order books are scaling earnings rapidly, compute **Forward P/E** by holding today's Market Cap (or Share Price) static and dividing by estimated future PAT (or EPS) **at most 2 years out** (forecasting beyond 2 years is overreach) \[[02:27:57–02:36:37\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8877)] \[PDF: image not legible — content taken from transcript\]: \$\$\\text{Forward P/E} \= \\frac{\\text{Current Market Cap}}{\\text{Estimated Future PAT (Year 1 or Year 2)}} \= \\frac{\\text{Current Stock Price}}{\\text{Estimated Future EPS}}\$\$ Charlie Munger noted he had never seen Warren Buffett build a formal spreadsheet DCF—instead, Buffett thinks in terms of **forward multiples** (e.g., if ₹4,000 crore market cap generates ₹500 crore PAT in 2 years, that is an **8x 2-year forward P/E**, or ₹10,000 crore market cap at a 20x exit multiple) \[[02:29:48–02:31:04\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8988)].

#### First-to-File One-Offs & The 4–5 Key Variables Filter

In pharmaceuticals, a **first-to-file** opportunity (6–12 months of exclusivity when a drug goes off patent, shared among early filers) generates massive 2–3 year windfall profits that disappear once competitors enter \[[02:37:09–02:39:15\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9429)]. Markets refuse to re-rate these temporary cash flows, leading to a **value trap / time correction** if no new cash-flow engine replaces them \[[02:38:28–02:39:56\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9508)].

Following Buffett's key-variables rule, every investment thesis must follow the sequence: **Variables → Valuation → Technicals** \[[02:39:56–02:42:19\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9596)]:

1. **Identify 4–5 Key Variables:** Only 4–5 variables truly drive a business's future earnings (where you can be right or wrong). If a business has too many uncontrollable variables, pass—or ride it strictly with a **V-Stop** technical stop-loss \[[02:39:56–02:42:19\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9596)].  
2. **Homework & Part 2 Preview:** Watch the 1–1.5 hour **Operating Leverage** video on YouTube before Part 2 (which will cover PEG, sectoral multiples, SOTP on Laurus Labs, PI Industries, contractors, hospitals, defense, new-age IPOs, EMS, packaging, and P2P analysis) \[[02:43:08–02:45:17\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9788)].

## 7\. GLOSSARY

- **Cornered Float:** Stock where \~90%+ of shares are locked with promoters/institutions, causing tiny daily volumes and artificially elevated P/E multiples (e.g., Divgi TorqTransfer Systems, Metro Brands) \[[02:18:39–02:22:00](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=8319), [02:54:39–02:55:25\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=10479)].  
- **De-Rating Event:** Contraction of a stock's P/E multiple due to negative perception, regulatory shocks, or slowing growth ("haunted house" analogy) \[[00:58:44–01:02:35](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=3524), [01:42:23\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=6143)].  
- **Earnings Yield:** Inverse of P/E (\$\\frac{1}{\\text{P/E}} \\times 100\$), representing annual percentage return on current price \[[01:19:25–01:21:00\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4765)].  
- **First-to-File Opportunity:** Pharma regulatory exclusivity (6–12 months) for the first generic filers on an off-patent drug, creating temporary 2–3 year windfalls (e.g., Natco Pharma's Revlimid) \[[02:37:09–02:39:15\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9429)].  
- **Forward P/E:** Current Market Cap divided by estimated PAT 1–2 years forward (holding market cap static) \[[02:31:04–02:36:37\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9064)].  
- **Intrinsic Value:** Fundamental worth of a business driven by future cash flows, earnings growth, and management ethics/governance \[[01:24:29–01:25:22](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5069), [01:28:33–01:29:56\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5313)].  
- **Overhang of Overpaying:** Multi-year sideways or negative stock performance caused by buying at 80x–230x P/E \[[01:07:57–01:14:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4077)].  
- **PEG Ratio:** P/E Ratio divided by expected EPS growth rate (%) \[[00:32:00–00:33:44\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=1920)].  
- **Perception-to-Earnings Ratio:** Speaker's framing of P/E as \$\\text{Perception (Transactional Value)} \\div \\text{Earnings (Intrinsic Value)}\$ \[[01:29:06–01:31:52\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5346)].  
- **Re-Rating Event:** Expansion of a stock's P/E multiple triggered by positive catalysts, order books, or sector tailwinds ("airport & metro" analogy) \[[01:00:42–01:05:18\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=3642)].  
- **SOTP (Sum of the Parts):** Used without definition (previewed for Part 2\) \[[01:14:52](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=4492), [02:44:05](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=9845), [03:11:13\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=11473)].  
- **Transactional Value:** Price driven solely by what the next buyer is willing to pay based on sentiment/heritage rather than cash flows (e.g., Mona Lisa, Bitcoin, Solana) \[[01:25:22–01:28:33\](/transcripts/cc/CC_Lec05?t=5122)].

## 11\. SELF-TEST

1. **Conceptual:** Why does Warren Buffett say valuation cannot be taught like real estate appraising?  
   - *Answer:* You only need 4–5 predictable key variables and rough accuracy ("fat pitch"), not 5-decimal precision; unpredictable businesses should be skipped (Stage 2).  
2. **Applied (Alkyl Amines):** At 12–13x P/E with 20% expected earnings growth from ₹300–₹400 Cr Acetonitrile Capex, what was the PEG ratio, and why was 75x P/E dangerous later?  
   - *Answer:* 0.6x PEG (60 paise per ₹1 growth) at entry; 75x P/E on peak 42% margins (vs. 17–21% average) risked simultaneous earnings and multiple collapse (Stage 3).  
3. **Applied (Pidilite):** If Pidilite returned 24% CAGR over 10 years while EPS grew only 11.79% CAGR, what drove the rest—and what happened afterward?  
   - *Answer:* P/E expansion (e.g., 30x → 100x), which later reversed into negative P/E CAGR (de-rating) (Stage 4).  
4. **Conceptual:** Explain "De-Rating" and "Re-Rating" using the Haunted House analogy.  
   - *Answer:* Negative perception ("ghost") drops a ₹2 Cr house to ₹50L (de-rating); an airport/metro announcement lifts it to ₹8–10 Cr (re-rating) (Stage 5).  
5. **Applied (Insecticides India):** With ₹1,800 Cr Market Cap and ₹63 Cr TTM PAT, what is the P/E and approximate Earnings Yield?  
   - *Answer:* \~28.56x P/E and \~3.5% Earnings Yield (Stage 6).  
6. **Conceptual:** How do Intrinsic Value and Transactional Value differ?  
   - *Answer:* Intrinsic value comes from cash flows, growth, and ethics (Tata Tech); transactional value comes from what the next buyer will pay (Mona Lisa, Bitcoin) (Stage 6).  
7. **Applied (Aarti Industries):** Reported PAT was ₹1,186 Cr (19x P/E at ₹26,000 Cr mcap), but included an ₹800 Cr one-off fee leaving ₹586 Cr core PAT. What was the true P/E?  
   - *Answer:* \~44.4x (\~45x) P/E (Stage 8).  
8. **Applied (Infosys):** Compare 5-year CAGRs for Infosys bought in 2000 (206x P/E) vs. 2002 (30x P/E).  
   - *Answer:* \-5% CAGR from 2000 vs. \+18% CAGR from 2002 (Stage 8).  
9. **Applied (Forward P/E):** A ₹100 stock has FY24 EPS of ₹3 (33.3x). If FY26 EPS reaches ₹8, what is the 2-year Forward P/E, and what is the stock price at a 30x exit multiple?  
   - *Answer:* 12.5x Forward P/E (`100 ÷ 8`); ₹240 (`30 × 8`) (Stage 9).  
10. **Conceptual:** Why did Natco Pharma stay at 10–13x P/E despite 4x PAT growth?  
    - *Answer:* First-to-file Revlimid exclusivity is a temporary 2–3 year windfall, not sustainable cash flow (Stage 9, 10).

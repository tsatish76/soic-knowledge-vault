## BF-Lec04 – Stage 1: Warrants as Aligned Promoter Incentives & Cyclical Caveat  \[00:00:00–00:03:42\]  \[TVGP: Promoter, Valuation\]

### a) What this stage answers

How do promoter warrant issuances signal upcoming 18-month tailwinds, and why can promoter buying still fail without cyclical base-rate checks and exit rules?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### Reading Warrant Threads as Incentive Signals

Opening with Power Mech (`"power make"` in STT `[?]`) and a 1.5-year-old tweet thread, the speaker shows how promoter warrant issuances work as an incentive-caused bias signal `[TVGP: Promoter]` \[00:00:00–00:01:23\]. When promoters foresee industry tailwinds over the next **18 months**, they move to increase their equity stake \[00:01:23–00:01:41\].

#### Warrant Mechanics

Suppose a stock trades at **₹200** and the promoter issues **2% warrants** worth **₹100 crore** \[00:01:41\]. The promoter locks in the **₹200** conversion price for the next **18 months**, pays **15%** upfront (`₹15 crore` into the company `[? verify]`), and must put in the remaining **85%** (`₹85 crore`) within 18 months \[00:01:41\]. When promoter incentives are aligned toward market-cap creation, major value-unlocking moves often follow \[00:01:01–00:01:41\].

#### The Cyclical Trap: Why Exit Rules Still Matter

To prevent blind imitation of promoter buying, the speaker pairs those successes with a cautionary counter-example: **HEG and Graphite India** \[00:01:41–00:03:42\] `[TVGP: Valuation]`. In 2017, HEG's promoter bought shares almost daily, and margins exploded over the next two years during the graphite electrode upcycle \[00:01:41–00:03:09\]. Market participants rationalized the peak by calling them *"specialty electrodes"* (citing ₹2,000–3,000 crore capex `"anodes of Kpex"` `[?]`) instead of checking long-term margin base rates \[00:01:41–00:03:09\]. Despite repeated warnings in 2018 from **Contrarian EPS** on Twitter, HEG and Graphite India crashed **90% from their peak** ("so much for the moat") \[00:03:09–00:03:42\]. Objective exit strategies, selling criteria, and system-level thinking are essential to remove EQ and protect gains \[00:03:09–00:03:42\].

## BF-Lec04 – Stage 3: Perverse Incentives — Vietnam Rat-Tail Case & Accounting Fudging  \[00:04:48–00:07:19\]

### a) What this stage answers

What are perverse incentives, and how do they cause small-cap promoters to fudge numbers?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### The Cobra / Rat-Tail Effect in French-Occupied Vietnam

Not all incentives produce healthy outcomes—some create **perverse incentives** (`"fervor incentives"` in STT `[?]`) \[00:04:48–00:05:56\]. During the French occupation of Vietnam, authorities wanted to reduce the rat population and offered a bounty for every **rat tail** turned in \[00:04:48–00:05:28\]. Instead of killing rats, Vietnamese residents cut off the tails (leaving rats alive to breed), smuggled foreign rats into the city, and actively bred rats to collect more bounties—causing the rat population to **increase** \[00:05:28–00:05:56\].

#### Market Parallel: Market-Cap Obsession & Accounting Fudging

In small-cap stocks, when promoters become obsessed with boosting market cap to get rewarded, perverse incentives lead to **fudging of numbers** \[00:05:28–00:05:56\]. That is why fundamental **checks and balances** (taught later in the crash course) are indispensable—everything in markets ultimately ties back to behavioural finance and human psychology \[00:05:56–00:07:19\].

\[00:06:33: non-content / timing note — speaker notes the live session has reached \~2:36 PM, \~100 minutes in, and praises engagement on behavioural finance.\]

### c) Examples & case studies

- **Example: French-Occupied Vietnam Rat-Tail Bounty**  \[00:04:48–00:05:56\] \[Slide not available\]  
  - **Context**: Classic historical illustration of perverse incentives.  
  - **Data/details**: Bounty paid per rat tail rather than dead rat → tail cutting, rat smuggling, and rat breeding → higher rat population.  
  - **Walkthrough**: Paying for the wrong proxy metric (tails) incentivized gaming the system rather than solving the problem.  
  - **Takeaway**: Whenever management is rewarded purely for short-term market cap or stock price, expect potential accounting manipulation; verify with fundamental checks.

### a) What this stage answers

Why are retail F\&O trading algos aggressively marketed on social media, and why does process quality beat short-term bull-market luck?

### b) Detailed explanation

#### The "Amazon of Algo Platforms" Incentive Trap

Applying incentive-caused bias to modern retail trading, the speaker warns against ads, emails, and Twitter threads selling computer-based **F\&O trading algos** \[00:12:29–00:13:16\]. #### Good Process vs. Bull-Market Luck

Speaker's view: Using third-party algos carries a high risk of blowing up at the portfolio level (`"non-english on PF level"` `[?]` in STT) \[00:14:01\]. Finance is a rough field driven by FOMO: if your **process is good**, you are likely to earn money over the long term; if your **process is bad**, you might still make money on 4–5 lucky trades during a bull market, only to lose money over the long run \[00:14:41–00:15:16\].

# Sub-Course Master Summary: TVGP: Behavioural Finance (BF)
## 1. OVERARCHING THEME & OBJECTIVE
The Behavioural Finance module (BF-Lec01–BF-Lec04) establishes emotional discipline (EQ) and cognitive self-awareness as the prerequisite foundation for equity investing before any fundamental, technical, or watchlist-building work begins (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02). Across four sessions, it trains investors to recognize when fast, evolutionary "System 1" reflexes hijack decisions—causing panic selling on headlines, linear return expectations, "physics envy" in valuation, FOMO at chart peaks or in F&O, and emotional attachment to stocks—and equips them with deliberate "System 2" checklists, portfolio-level anchoring, and objective exit rules (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02, BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04).

Within the TVGP (Theme, Valuation, Growth, Promoter) framework, this module touches all four pillars while placing its heaviest analytical weight on **Promoter** and **Valuation** (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02, BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04). **Promoter** alignment is examined through insider buying during rumors (BF-Lec01), skin in the game via shareholding, ESOPs, and warrants (BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04), and the warning against perverse incentives and cyclical traps (BF-Lec04). **Valuation** is framed around rejecting false precision in favour of rough valuation zones, extreme multiple checks, Reverse DCF at FOMO peaks, and long-term margin base rates (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02, BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04), while **Theme** and **Growth** appear through localized real-estate tailwinds (BF-Lec02), 5-year unlisted scaling (BF-Lec03), capex/CWIP triangulation (BF-Lec04), and India's PLI policy chain (BF-Lec04).

## 3. CORE PILLARS OF THE MODULE
### Pillar 1: System 1 vs. System 2 & Emotional Discipline (EQ)
**Why it matters**: Without controlling fast, automatic System 1 ("reptile brain") reactions, fundamental and technical skill cannot compound because investors get shaken out during routine drawdowns (BF-Lec01). Deliberate System 2 thinking enables probabilistic, process-driven decisions (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02).
**Progression across lectures**: BF-Lec01 (introduced via reading, math, and visual illusions + crash checklist) → BF-Lec02 (formalized via *Thinking, Fast and Slow* and applied to bottom-up sector research) → BF-Lec03 (paired with a checklist to stop FOMO pitches).
**Key rules/thresholds**: 85%–90% triangle skimming error rate (BF-Lec01); 50%–60% bat-and-ball error rate (BF-Lec01); Day 47 vs. Day 24 lily pad exponential check (BF-Lec02).
**Read more**: BF-Lec01 – Stages 1–3, 5, 7; BF-Lec02 – Stages 5–7; BF-Lec03 – Stage 4.
### Pillar 2: Portfolio-Level Anchoring & Positive Re-Anchoring
**Why it matters**: Retail investors fixate on single red positions or unrealized peak profits while ignoring total portfolio performance, or anchor to meaningless nominal numbers like sub-₹100 share prices and ₹10 NFO NAVs (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec03). Re-anchoring to systems and process eliminates Endowment Bias (BF-Lec03).
**Progression across lectures**: BF-Lec01 (shift anchor from individual losers to aggregate portfolio P&L) → BF-Lec03 (expose price, tax, NFO, and shrinkflation anchors; flip anchoring positively onto exit rules, buying criteria, valuations, and price-volume action).
**Key rules/thresholds**: Anchor to 5-year+ Indian market base rates across 20–35 years (BF-Lec01); avoid anchoring to unrealized peak MTM (e.g., ₹15 lakh) or raw *bhaav* (BF-Lec03).
**Read more**: BF-Lec01 – Stages 4, 7; BF-Lec03 – Stages 5–6.
### Pillar 3: Rough Valuation Zones & Objective Exit Systems
**Why it matters**: Because market participants are irrational, finance is 50%–60% art and 40%–50% science—making exact intrinsic-value precision ("physics envy") impossible (BF-Lec02). Rough valuation awareness paired with objective technical exit rules protects gains during both crashes and parabolic peaks (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02, BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04).
**Progression across lectures**: BF-Lec01 (crash hold/exit rule via valuations + 30-WMA / V-Stop) → BF-Lec02 (anti-physics envy, rough extreme-valuation checks) → BF-Lec03 (200 EMA >70%–80% stretch + Reverse DCF peak detector) → BF-Lec04 (cyclical margin base-rate guard against -90% crashes).
**Key rules/thresholds**: Hold crashes if valuations are cheap and 30-WMA / V-Stop is untriggered (BF-Lec01); DMart ~279x P/E [? verify] / >5 SD above retail peers (BF-Lec02); >70%–80% (especially >80%) above 200 EMA signals an "Eiffel Tower" peak (BF-Lec03).
**Read more**: BF-Lec01 – Stage 7; BF-Lec02 – Stages 3–4; BF-Lec03 – Stage 2; BF-Lec04 – Stage 1.
### Pillar 4: FOMO Defense & Base-Rate Grounding
**Why it matters**: Super Deprival Reaction Syndrome drives FOMO in overextended equities, F&O trading, and hyped SMEs/microcaps (BF-Lec03), as well as retail algo platforms (BF-Lec04). Grounding decisions in base rates prevents capital blow-ups (BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04).
**Progression across lectures**: BF-Lec01 (5-year Indian equity base rates) → BF-Lec03 (SEBI 89%–90% F&O loss base rate + checklist/"buffet of opportunities" defense) → BF-Lec04 (retail algo-platform FOMO vs. Graviton).
**Key rules/thresholds**: 89%–90% of retail F&O traders lose money; NSE derives 80%–85% of revenue from options/F&O; Zerodha earns ~₹2,000 Cr PAT (BF-Lec03).
**Read more**: BF-Lec01 – Stage 7; BF-Lec03 – Stages 1–4; BF-Lec04 – Stage 5.
### Pillar 5: Incentive Alignment, Skin in the Game & Policy Tailwinds
**Why it matters**: Incentives predict corporate, auditor, and policy outcomes (BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04). Tracking promoter stake, warrants, and capex reveals genuine conviction, while recognizing perverse incentives prevents accounting-fudging and cyclical traps (BF-Lec04).
**Progression across lectures**: BF-Lec01 (promoter 3%–4% buying contradicting Pricol rumors) → BF-Lec03 (auditor 31% bias, 80%–100% stake vs. 2%–3% / zero ESOPs, pre-tailwind warrants) → BF-Lec04 (18-month warrant mechanics, open-market + CWIP/AGM triangulation, perverse incentives, 4-level PLI thinking).
**Key rules/thresholds**: Auditor bias +31% when paid by company (BF-Lec03); high promoter stake (80%–100%) vs. low (2%–3% / zero ESOPs) (BF-Lec03); warrants 15% upfront / 85% over 18 months [? verify] (BF-Lec04); doubling FA/CWIP + 3x capacity (BF-Lec04).
**Read more**: BF-Lec01 – Stage 6; BF-Lec03 – Stage 7; BF-Lec04 – Stages 1–4.

## 5. MASTER FRAMEWORK / CHECKLIST
**Pre-Commit to Process & EQ (****BF-Lec01****, ****BF-Lec02****)**: Accept that finance is 50%–60% art and 40%–50% science, expect lumpy returns across 5-year+ Indian market base rates, and anchor to total portfolio P&L rather than individual losers.
**Pause System 1 Before Reacting (****BF-Lec01****, ****BF-Lec02****, ****BF-Lec03****)**: Slow down on sensational macro headlines, Telegram rumors, or urgent SME/microcap/algo pitches; verify entity separation (e.g., broking vs. lending) and remember the market is a "buffet of opportunities."
**Apply 4-Level Bottom-Up Filtering (****BF-Lec02****, ****BF-Lec04****)**: Spot sector tailwinds (52-week highs or India PLI incentives), reject diluted pan-India exposure where local cycles diverge, and trace 3rd–4th level value-chain bottlenecks using System 2 bull/bear/base scenarios.
**Audit Incentives & Capex Triangulation (****BF-Lec01****, ****BF-Lec03****, ****BF-Lec04****)**: Check promoter shareholding (high skin in the game vs. 2%–3% / zero ESOPs), pre-tailwind warrants (18-month lock), and open-market buying corroborated by doubling fixed assets/CWIP and AGM capacity expansion; screen out perverse market-cap incentives.
**Validate Valuation Zone & Cyclical Base Rates (****BF-Lec02****, ****BF-Lec03****, ****BF-Lec04****)**: Judge rough valuation zones (flagging extremes like >5 SD above peers or absurd Reverse DCF expectations) and cross-check booming margins against long-term historical margin trends.
**Execute Objective Entry & Exit Rules (****BF-Lec01****, ****BF-Lec03****)**: Act as an unemotional "part-time business owner"—hold through crashes if valuations are cheap and 30-WMA / V-Stop is untriggered, and trim/exit parabolic FOMO peaks (>70%–80% above 200 EMA with selling volume).

## 8. COMMON MISTAKES & RED FLAGS ACROSS THE MODULE
**Letting System 1 react to headlines, rumors, and short-term price drops**: Panic selling unrelated group entities (IIFL Finance), reacting to Telegram rumors near moving averages (Pricol), or trading sensational macro/media narratives (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec02).
**Chasing FOMO in overextended charts, F&O, SMEs, and retail algos**: Buying >80% above 200 EMA ("Eiffel Tower"), trading F&O against 89%–90% loss odds on fake P&L screenshots, or buying algos from immature traders (BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04).
**False precision ("physics envy") & linear return expectations**: Demanding exact fair values in an art-heavy market or expecting straight-line bull-market compounding (BF-Lec02).
**Harmful anchoring & Endowment Bias**: Fixating on a single -3% to -4% loser, sub-₹100 share prices, ₹10 NFO NAVs, LTCG tax thresholds, ₹15L unrealized profits, or falling in love with stocks ("a stock won't love you back") (BF-Lec01, BF-Lec03).
**Misreading incentives & cyclical peaks**: Backing low-stake (2%–3%) / zero-ESOP managements, ignoring perverse market-cap accounting fudging, or mistaking cyclical margin booms (HEG/Graphite India -90%) for permanent moats (BF-Lec03, BF-Lec04).


## 9. THE MASTER TAKEAWAY
Completing TVGP: Behavioural Finance equips an investor to anchor to aggregate portfolio returns and 5-year+ base rates, pause System 1 impulses with deliberate System 2 checklists, reject valuation precision in favour of rough valuation zones paired with objective technical exit rules (V-Stop, 30-WMA, 200 EMA stretch), and filter opportunities through aligned promoter incentives (warrants, open-market buying, capex/CWIP) and 4-level policy thinking before moving into fundamentals and technicals.

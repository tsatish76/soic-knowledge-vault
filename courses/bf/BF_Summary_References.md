## BF-Lec01: System 1 Can Shake You Out: Behavioural Finance, Portfolio-Level Thinking, and Objective Exit Rules

Duration: 00:26:52 \| TVGP focus: Promoter, Valuation Detailed report: BF\_Lec01\_Report.md

### 1\. Core thesis

Fundamental and technical stock selection fails if an investor lacks the emotional discipline (EQ) to hold winners through routine drawdowns. Fast, evolutionary "System 1" reactions trigger panic selling on misinterpreted news and rumors, so investors must replace knee-jerk impulses with portfolio-level anchoring, deliberate System 2 verification, and objective exit rules.

### 2\. Frameworks & methods

- **Prevention-first wisdom**: Identify cognitive traps before capital is lost (Charlie Munger / Peter Bevelin `[?]`) rather than reacting post-error. → `BF-Lec01 – Stage 2`  
- **System 1 vs. System 2 awareness**: Recognize that fast pattern-matching fires first and dominates perception, whereas multi-step math and fact checks require deliberate System 2 effort. → `BF-Lec01 – Stage 3`  
- **Portfolio-level anchoring**: Shift emotional focus from individual losing lines to aggregate portfolio P\&L so one small red position does not dictate decisions. → `BF-Lec01 – Stage 4`  
- **Objective buy/sell & crash checklist**: Replace "reptile brain" panic during market crashes by checking valuations, portfolio business quality, and objective exit triggers (30-weekly moving average / V-Stop). → `BF-Lec01 – Stage 5 & Stage 7`

### 3\. Rules, thresholds & formulas

| Rule/Formula | Value/Condition | Stage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Triangle skimming error rate | 85%–90% missed duplicated words (`THE`, `A`, `THE`) | Stage 2 |
| Bat-and-ball cognitive check | `Bat + Ball = ₹110`, `₹100` diff → `₹5` (50%–60% wrongly picked `₹10`) | Stage 3 |
| Promoter stake conviction | 3%–4% promoter buying contradicted bearish rumors (Pricol) | Stage 6 |
| Crash hold/exit rule | Hold if valuations remain cheap and 30-WMA / V-Stop is untriggered | Stage 7 |
| Indian market base rate | Every 5-year horizon over 20–35 years produced strong returns | Stage 7 |

### 4\. Key examples

- **Joint family portfolio (16–17 stocks)** → Family fixated on 1 position down \-3% to \-4% despite broad profits, proving loss fixation → `Stage 4` `[00:13:39–00:16:16]`  
- **India vs. Pakistan cricket** → Virat Kohli scored 100, yet India lost all out for 330 chasing 350, proving aggregate team score beats one multi-bagger → `Stage 4` `[00:16:16–00:17:43]`  
- **IIFL Securities vs. IIFL Finance** → SEBI front-running ban on broking arm caused panic selling in lending arm, which rallied \~30% (`₹470 → ₹616` `[? verify]`) → `Stage 6` `[00:19:48–00:22:25]`  
- **Pricol vs. Minda rumor** → Dropped \~12% near 21-WMA on zero news while promoters bought 3%–4% stake, then rallied \~50% → `Stage 6` `[00:23:03–00:24:48]`

### 5\. Cautions & red flags

- Conflating separate group entities under a shared brand name during regulatory news `[00:21:20–00:22:25]`.  
- Acting on Telegram or social-media rumors when stocks drop 5%–12% on no news near moving averages `[00:23:41–00:24:48]`.  
- Judging decisions by short-term price outcomes (outcome bias) rather than slowing down `[00:22:25–00:23:03]`.  
- Letting the "reptile brain" force emotional exits during crashes when V-Stop/30-WMA is untriggered `[00:25:49–00:26:52]`.

### 6\. Decisive takeaway

Anchor to total portfolio returns and 5-year+ Indian market base rates, and slow down to run objective checks on valuations, business quality, and promoter actions before reacting. Only execute exits when pre-defined rules like the 30-weekly moving average or V-Stop are actually triggered.

## BF-Lec02: System 2 Will Clear the Air: Narrative Traps, False Precision ("Physics Envy"), and Bottom-Up Research

Duration: 00:25:49 \| TVGP focus: Theme, Valuation Detailed report: BF\_Lec02\_Report.md

### 1\. Core thesis

Media narratives chase price action rather than fundamentals, and bull markets breed a false expectation of smooth, linear returns. Because markets are driven by irrational humans, finance is 50%–60% art and 40%–50% science—requiring investors to reject "physics envy," use rough valuation ranges with technical exit systems, and apply deliberate System 2 bottom-up research.

### 2\. Frameworks & methods

- **Narrative skepticism**: Recognize that media outlets manufacture drama and flip narratives immediately after price reversals, so macro scares should not trigger System 1 exits. → `BF-Lec02 – Stage 1`  
- **Range-based valuation ("anti-physics envy")**: Estimate broad valuation zones (cheap vs. very expensive) rather than chasing exact intrinsic-value precision, pairing rough judgment with technical exit rules. → `BF-Lec02 – Stages 3–4`  
- **System 1 vs. System 2 (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*)**: Reserve fast, automatic System 1 for daily habits and engage slow, effortful System 2 for complex investing analysis. → `BF-Lec02 – Stage 6`  
- **Localized bottom-up sector filtering**: When a sector hits 52-week highs, avoid diluted pan-India exposure and drill down into specific micro-markets and companies. → `BF-Lec02 – Stage 7`

### 3\. Rules, thresholds & formulas

| Rule/Formula | Value/Condition | Stage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Composition of finance | 50%–60% art, 40%–50% science | Stage 4 |
| Extreme retail valuation (DMart) | \~279x P/E \[? verify\]; \>5 SD / 5x above retail peers | Stage 4 |
| Sector tailwind signal | Broad sector charts hitting 52-week highs | Stage 7 |
| System 2 company toolkit | Bull/Bear/Base cases, forward P/E \[?\], risks, thesis pointers | Stage 7 |

### 4\. Key examples

- **2016–2017 Donald Trump & S\&P 500** → S\&P 500 fell on anti-trade fears, recovered the same day, and media flipped to "pro-business" → `Stage 1` `[00:02:35–00:03:46]`  
- **Sunny-day tree & 10–15 yr investors** → Minds imagine a perfect tree, whereas real compounding is lumpy with troughs and monsoons → `Stage 2` `[00:05:53–00:10:31]`  
- **APL \[?\] & DMart** → APL re-rated from 12–17x (2020) to \~65x P/E; DMart hit \~279x P/E \[? verify\], proving rough valuation \+ exit systems beat exact models → `Stage 4` `[00:15:40–00:18:29]`  
- **Lily pad lake puzzle** → Doubling daily to fill a lake in 48 days means Day 47 is half-full, exposing linear Day-24 traps → `Stage 5` `[00:20:30–00:22:06]`  
- **Real estate bottom-up selection** → On 52-week-high tailwinds, bypassed pan-India players for Delhi (special situation) and Mumbai (highest appreciation) → `Stage 7` `[00:23:49–00:25:49]`

### 5\. Cautions & red flags

- Trading on sensational macro headlines (Hindenburg, war, SVB, H1B bans) that media businesses amplify `[00:00:00–00:04:34]`.  
- Expecting straight-line post-2017 bull-market returns instead of lumpy multi-year compounding `[00:05:53–00:10:31]`.  
- Suffering from "physics envy" or falling in love with stocks without technical exit rules `[00:18:29–00:20:30]`.  
- Buying pan-India sector leaders blindly without checking divergent local city cycles `[00:24:27–00:25:10]`.

### 6\. Decisive takeaway

Accept that finance is an imperfect art—50%–60% art and 40%–50% science—and use rough valuation zones paired with objective exit systems. Apply slow System 2 thinking to build bull/bear/base cases and isolate localized, bottom-up winners within sector tailwinds.

## BF-Lec03: Do You Suffer from FOMO? Super Deprival Reaction, Anchor Bias, Endowment Bias, and Incentive-Caused Bias

Duration: 00:28:06 \| TVGP focus: Valuation, Promoter, Growth Detailed report: BF\_Lec03\_Report.md

### 1\. Core thesis

Retail investors routinely destroy capital through four behavioural traps: FOMO (driven by Super Deprival Reaction Syndrome), destructive Anchor Bias, emotional Endowment Bias, and ignoring Incentive-Caused Bias. Protecting your wallet requires slowing down with a checklist, re-anchoring to objective systems and valuations, and backing businesses where promoters hold meaningful skin in the game.

### 2\. Frameworks & methods

- **FOMO peak & exit detector**: Flag multi-year peaks when price stretches \>70%–80% above the 200 EMA ("Eiffel Tower") alongside heavy distribution volume, absurd Reverse DCF expectations, and euphoric media headlines. → `BF-Lec03 – Stage 2`  
- **Checklist & "buffet of opportunities" filter**: Counter manufactured SME/microcap FOMO by slowing System 1, running a checklist, and skipping any business you do not understand. → `BF-Lec03 – Stage 4`  
- **Positive re-anchoring & "part-time owner" mindset**: Anchor to buying criteria, valuations, tailwinds, price-volume action, and exit triggers—not unrealized profits or raw *bhaav*—so exits happen without Endowment Bias. → `BF-Lec03 – Stage 6`  
- **Incentive alignment audit**: Evaluate promoter shareholding, executive ESOPs, and pre-tailwind warrant issuances to separate hungry operators from *"chalta hai"* managers. → `BF-Lec03 – Stage 7`

### 3\. Rules, thresholds & formulas

| Rule/Formula | Value/Condition | Stage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 200 EMA peak stretch | Price \>70%–80% (especially \>80%) above 200 EMA | Stage 2 |
| SEBI F\&O loss base rate | 89%–90% of retail F\&O traders lose money | Stage 3 |
| Broker / exchange F\&O capture | Zerodha \~₹2,000 Cr PAT; NSE 80%–85% options revenue | Stage 3 |
| Auditor incentive bias | 31% higher acceptance of dubious accounting for company | Stage 7 |
| Skin-in-the-game benchmark | High (80%–100% stake / warrants) vs. Low (2%–3%, 0 ESOPs) | Stage 7 |

### 4\. Key examples

- **IRCTC** → \>80% above 200 EMA ("Eiffel Tower"), selling volume, Reverse DCF excess, and "500% PSU" articles marked a multi-year peak → `Stage 2` `[00:03:49–00:06:08]`  
- **₹1.9 Cr P\&L fraud & *Wolf of Wall Street*** → Fake F\&O screenshots and Aerotyne penny-stock urgency manufacture FOMO → `Stages 3–4` `[00:08:04, 00:11:10–00:13:07]`  
- **Ramdeo Agarwal / Pentamedia \[?\] & Lay's ₹10** → Tax anchoring delayed exits; Lay's shrank packet content over 10 yrs (6%–7% inflation) to preserve ₹10 anchor → `Stage 5` `[00:15:55–00:19:00]`  
- **Warren Buffett (Dexter Shoes, Chevron, Airlines)** → \$6–8B share-swap loss; sold Chevron/Airlines at a loss without Endowment Bias → `Stage 6` `[00:21:08–00:22:13]`  
- **Unlisted mfg (`Nightway` \[?\]) & financier vs. low-stake listed firm** → 100% family stake grew ₹100→₹1,000 Cr sales in 5 yrs; 80% financier enforced daily collection stops; 2%–3% stake \+ zero ESOPs bred complacency → `Stage 7` `[00:24:02–00:28:06]`

### 5\. Cautions & red flags

- Chasing F\&O on social-media P\&L screenshots when 89%–90% lose money `[00:06:54–00:09:07]`.  
- Buying stocks simply for sub-₹100 prices or ₹10 NFO NAVs, or holding peaks for tax reasons `[00:14:35–00:17:31]`.  
- Falling in love with stocks ("a stock won't love you back") or anchoring to ₹15L unrealized profits `[00:19:45–00:21:08]`.  
- Backing companies with 2%–3% promoter holding and zero executive ESOPs `[00:24:58–00:28:06]`.

### 6\. Decisive takeaway

Never chase FOMO in overextended charts (\>80% above 200 EMA) or F\&O—anchor instead to your checklist, valuations, and exit triggers as an unemotional part-time business owner. Always verify that promoter incentives, shareholding, and ESOPs or warrants are aligned with long-term compounding.

## BF-Lec04: The Power of Incentives: Warrants, Perverse Incentives, 4-Level PLI Thinking, and Course Roadmap

Duration: 00:20:57 \| TVGP focus: Promoter, Growth, Theme, Valuation Detailed report: BF\_Lec04\_Report.md

### 1\. Core thesis

Incentives drive corporate, policy, and market-intermediary behaviour—creating multi-baggers when promoters issue warrants or buy shares ahead of capex, and disasters when perverse incentives trigger accounting fudging or algo-selling hype. Pairing incentive signals with long-term margin base rates, 4-level value-chain thinking, and holistic "bullshit filters" protects investors from cyclical and behavioural traps.

### 2\. Frameworks & methods

- **Warrant & open-market triangulation**: Track 18-month promoter warrants and open-market buying alongside balance-sheet fixed assets, CWIP, and AGM capacity disclosures to verify insider conviction. → `BF-Lec04 – Stages 1–2`  
- **Cyclical base-rate & exit guard**: Cross-check booming margins against long-term historical margin trends and enforce objective exit systems even when promoters bought aggressively. → `BF-Lec04 – Stage 1`  
- **4-level policy-incentive thinking**: Trace national incentives (India's PLI scheme, *How Asia Works*) from broad sector growth down to niche, peerless component bottlenecks. → `BF-Lec04 – Stage 4`  
- **Holistic "bullshit filter" process**: Combine moats, fundamentals, valuations, technicals, 6–7 position-sizing strategies, exits, and management analysis across the 24–25 hour course. → `BF-Lec04 – Stage 7`

Use promoter warrants, open-market buying, and government PLI incentives as high-signal filters—always verified against balance-sheet CWIP, perverse-incentive checks, and long-term margin base rates. Build a complete, multi-pillar investing process to filter out FOMO, broker/F\&O hype, and storyfication.

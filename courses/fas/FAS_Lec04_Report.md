## 2. KEY LEARNINGS
- **The "NEW" Word & Rate-of-Change Core:** Filtering across 5,000+ listed stocks starts by hunting companies doing something *new* (new product, capex, geography, client, or industry cycle) because fundamental analysis is the study of rate of change, where trends often last 3–5 years [0:04:11–0:08:28].
- **CANSLIM Indian Earnings Threshold:** Under the "C" (Current Big/Accelerating Quarterly Earnings & Sales), screen Indian companies delivering **>15% sales growth** and **>20% PAT growth**; big earnings breakouts typically persist for **4–6 quarters** [0:09:35, 0:22:59].
- **B2B/B2G Bullwhip vs. B2C Saturation:** With basic Indian FMCG/B2C categories >95% penetrated (e.g., toothpaste), focus on B2B/B2G cycles where multi-year order books and government capex (e.g., ₹9.2 lakh cr National Electricity Plan, Kavach) drive 2–3+ year earnings visibility [0:11:15–0:16:34].
- **15–30x P/E Screening Sweet Spot:** After screening for strong quarterly growth, prioritize businesses trading at **15–30x P/E** (or **15–35x** in richer markets) so P/E rerating remains on the table alongside earnings growth [0:33:15–0:36:50].
- **Ignore Delivery % on High-Volume Breakouts:** High-volume institutional accumulation days often show a *lower* delivery percentage (e.g., 20% of 10,000 = 2,000 shares delivered vs. 50% of 1,000 = 500 shares), so track absolute price-volume action rather than delivery % [0:57:30–0:59:14].
- **MarketSmith India Two-Screen Playbook (1% Learning Rule):** Allocate at least **1% of portfolio value** to learning/tools; use **LCA (Laggard-to-Leader)** after market corrections and **Growth 50 / Blue Dot** (EPS + RS ratings) to spot sector leaders [1:02:19–1:12:11].
- **Institutional Sponsorship & Promoter Buying Screens:** Screen on Screener.in for **1Y/3Y FII + DII holding increase >3%** or **Promoter holding increase >1–2%** paired with 52-week highs / Relative Strength [1:28:03–1:35:25].
- **Relative Strength as a Fundamental Signal:** Since **37%** of a stock's move comes from its industry group and **12%** from its sector (~49% combined, and 60%+ of big winners move in groups), 26-week and 21-day Relative Strength against Nifty 50 surfaces fundamental earnings leaders early [1:13:35–1:15:43, 1:55:19, 2:03:31] [Slide 23–24].
- **Free "Cheat Code" Google Alerts:** Set free Google Alerts on marquee investors (e.g., Ashish Kacholia) and track bulk/block deals and sectoral experts on X/Twitter to capture high-conviction ideas instantaneously [2:06:52–2:09:14].

## FAS-Lec04 – Stage 1: Why Stock Filtration Matters & The "NEW" Catalyst Framework [0:00:10–0:08:28] [TVGP: Theme, Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
Why is structured stock filtration essential across a 5,000+ stock universe, and what is the single unifying catalyst ("NEW") that drives multi-year fundamental rate-of-change?
### b) Detailed explanation
#### Cutting Through Market Noise to Become Self-Sufficient
The session aims to teach a repeatable stock-filtration philosophy, sector rotation, and individual stock-scanning methods—not stock tips or buy/sell recommendations (`[0:00:10–0:01:23]`, `[Slide 2]`). With over 5,000 listed stocks in India, investors are constantly bombarded by noise from WhatsApp forwards, Twitter/X posts, and random tips (`[0:01:50–0:02:16]`). Having explicit filtration criteria and mental models narrows those 5,000 needles down to a focused subset where the probability of winning is meaningfully higher (`[0:02:16]`).
#### The Unifying Word: "NEW" & Fundamental Rate of Change
When examining major winners across recent cycles—such as Shaily Engineering, PDS Ltd, HBL Engineering, Kernex Microsystems, Kaynes Technology, Garware Hi-Tech Films, Garware Technical Fibres, PG Electroplast, Danish Power, and Welspun Corp—the common thread is not merely "tailwinds" or "Stage 2," but one word: **NEW** (`[0:02:16–0:04:11]`). Every major catalyst begins with something new (`[0:07:19–0:07:53]`):

- **New products:** Shaily Engineering launching EpiPens for GLP-1 drugs; Garware Hi-Tech launching Paint Protection Films (PPF) three years earlier (`[0:04:11–0:05:26]`).
- **New commercialization / segments:** HBL Engineering commercializing Kavach railway safety systems post-2021 and executing locomotive orders; Kaynes Technology expanding into semiconductors and EMS; pharma companies entering CDMO after 2016–2017 (`[0:04:11–0:06:44]`).
- **New geographies & clients:** PDS Ltd entering the US geography and winning new US orders (`[0:04:52–0:05:26]`).
- **New capex & regulations:** Indian specialty chemical companies putting up new capex and new products during China's 2012–2021 chemical plant shutdowns (a 9-year trend); Borosil Renewables benefiting from anti-dumping duty regulations on solar glass alongside new capex (`[0:06:06–0:07:19]`).
#### Fundamental Analysis as the Study of Rate of Change
Fundamental analysis is the study of **rate of change** in a business and its industry, not merely accounting or forensic auditing (`[0:05:26–0:06:06]`). While valuations matter over the long term in India, over a 1–3 year investing horizon the decisive factor is getting the catalyst ("NEW") right (`[0:07:19–0:07:53]`). Stock filtration comes first; position sizing (4%, 6%, or 8% weights) and final stock picking come later (`[0:07:53–0:08:28]`).
### c) Examples & case studies
- **Example:** Shaily Engineering (`[0:02:16, 0:04:11]`)
  - Context: Illustrating the "new product" catalyst.
  - Data/details: Developing/manufacturing EpiPens for healthcare / GLP-1 drugs.
  - Takeaway: A new high-value product line initiates a multi-year rate-of-change cycle.
- **Example:** HBL Engineering & Kernex (`[0:02:53, 0:04:11]`)
  - Context: Illustrating new segment commercialization.
  - Data/details: HBL commercialized Kavach post-2021 and moved into locomotive order execution.
  - Takeaway: Multi-year railway safety capex creates sustained order visibility.
- **Example:** Garware Hi-Tech Films & PDS Ltd (`[0:02:53, 0:04:52]`)
  - Context: Illustrating new products (Garware Hi-Tech PPF launched 3 years ago) and new geographies (PDS winning orders in the US).
  - Takeaway: Both product innovation and geographic expansion act as "NEW" filtration triggers.
- **Example:** Indian Chemicals (2012–2021) & Borosil Renewables (`[0:06:06–0:07:19]`)
  - Context: Demonstrating how long "NEW" industry cycles can last.
  - Data/details: China's chemical shutdown drove a 9-year Indian chemical upcycle (2012–2021); anti-dumping duty + new capex acted as triggers for solar glass.
  - Takeaway: Industry-level rate of change often persists for 3–5+ years.

## FAS-Lec04 – Stage 2: CANSLIM "C": Big/Accelerating Earnings, B2B/B2G Cycles & Value Traps [0:08:28–0:22:15] [TVGP: Theme, Growth, Valuation]
### a) What this stage answers
How does William O'Neil's CANSLIM "C" criterion translate to the Indian market, why do B2B/B2G businesses dominate multi-year winners over saturated B2C staples, and how does GARP investing avoid deep-value traps?
### b) Detailed explanation
#### CANSLIM "C" & Momentum of Fundamentals
Drawing on William O'Neil's *How to Make Money in Stocks* (`[0:08:59]`, `[Slide 6–8]`), **C** stands for **Current Big or Accelerating Quarterly Earnings and Sales Growth** (`[0:08:59–0:09:35]`):

- **O'Neil's US Benchmark:** Current quarterly EPS up 25–50% YoY (with top winners showing 100–500%+), and 3 out of 4 biggest winners averaging >70% earnings growth in the latest reported quarter before major advances (`[Slide 6]`).
- **Indian Screening Threshold:** Screen for **>15% sales growth** and **>20% PAT growth** (`[0:09:35]`).
- **Momentum of Fundamentals:** Companies that post strong quarters tend to continue posting strong quarters, and companies that stumble tend to continue struggling—a ballpark thumb rule that holds roughly **60–70% of the time** (`[0:09:35–0:11:15]`).
#### Why B2B & B2G Dominate Over Saturated B2C Staples
In India, basic consumer staples (B2C/FMCG) like toothpaste (>95% penetration), soaps, and shaving have reached near-full TAM penetration, leaving growth reliant on low single-digit replacement demand (e.g., 4–5% in mature segments) (`[0:11:15–0:12:55]`). Consequently, most multi-year winners over the past 4–10 years have been **B2B** (selling to other businesses) and **B2G** (winning government tenders) (`[0:12:55–0:13:47]`):

- **B2B Bullwhip (Stocking & De-stocking):** When US retail demand is strong or corporates rush to digitize (e.g., Indian IT between 2020–2022), buyers over-order to avoid stockouts, creating multi-year stocking booms followed by de-stocking lulls (`[0:13:47–0:15:22]`).
- **B2G Tender Waves:** Government tenders arrive in clusters—for example, Kavach orders flowing sequentially to CG Power, Kernex, HBL Power, and unlisted Medha, or transformer orders (Shilchar [?], TRIL) driven by India's **₹9.2 lakh crore National Electricity Plan** for transmission & distribution connecting remote solar plants to cities (`[0:15:22–0:17:08]`).
#### Deep Value Traps vs. GARP (Growth as "Steroids" for Valuations)
- **Value Traps:** Buying statistically cheap stocks without growth requires immense patience; a value stock remains a value stock until growth arrives (`[0:17:08–0:19:40]`). For example, Shilchar [?] built multi-year capex in 2017–2018 during a 2015–2020 downcycle—remaining statistically cheap for five years without demand (`[0:18:02–0:19:09]`).
- **GARP Discipline:** Growth acts like "steroids" injected into valuations—driving both earnings growth and P/E rerating (`[0:17:37]`, `[Slide 7]`). Even as a disciplined GARP investor tracking quarterly/annual growth, hitting **60–70% accuracy** is more than enough because regulatory bans or margin collapses inevitably happen (`[0:19:09–0:19:40]`).
#### Why India Produces More Multibaggers Than China
Despite China's massive economic size, the Shanghai Composite went roughly flat over 15 years because Chinese state-owned enterprises (SOEs) prioritize production and employment over return ratios, resulting in **8–9% average corporate ROE** (`[0:20:16–0:21:57]`). In contrast, Indian corporates focus heavily on return metrics, generating **14–15% average cash ROE** alongside fast economic growth—fueling multibaggers (`[0:21:57–0:22:15]`).
### c) Examples & case studies
- **Example:** Cisco, Dell & America Online (AOL) (`[Slide 7]`)
  - Context: William O'Neil's US case studies proving booming profits drive booming stock prices (`Stock Returns = Earnings Growth + P/E Rerating`).
  - Data/details: Cisco EPS +150% and +155% (two quarters ending Oct 1990) before its 3-year run-up; Dell EPS +74% and +108% prior to Nov 1996 advance; AOL earnings +900% and +283% before Oct 1998 burst.
- **Example:** Indian IT (2020–2022) & Transformers / Kavach (`[0:14:27, 0:15:40–0:17:08]`)
  - Context: B2B front-loaded corporate digitization and B2G tender clustering.
  - Data/details: ₹9.2 lakh cr National Electricity Plan T&D capex driving transformer demand; sequential Kavach tenders across CG Power, Kernex, HBL, and Medha.
- **Example:** Shilchar [?] (2015–2020 Downcycle) (`[0:18:02–0:19:09]`)
  - Context: Illustrating the risk of deep-value investing without a growth inflection.
  - Data/details: Capex completed in 2017–2018, but 2015–2020 industry downcycle delayed returns for years.

## FAS-Lec04 – Stage 3: Free Quarterly Growth Screening on Screener.in [0:22:15–0:45:23] [TVGP: Growth, Valuation]
### a) What this stage answers
How do you build and run a free Screener.in query to surface high-growth Indian businesses at reasonable valuations, and what accounting or business-model exceptions must you watch for?
### b) Detailed explanation
#### The 5–6 Quarter Earnings Explosion Window
Big winners almost always share an explosion in quarterly and annual earnings growth, which typically lasts **5 to 6 quarters** before a higher base slows YoY comparisons—unless fresh capex extends the runway (`[0:22:15–0:23:36]`).
#### Free Screener.in Query Construction
Speaker's live Screener.in query (`[0:23:36–0:24:21]`, `[0:41:57–0:42:52]`):

- `YoY Quarterly Sales Growth > 15%`
- `YoY Quarterly Profit (PAT) Growth > 20%`
- `ROCE > 15%` *(Note: drop the ROCE filter when hunting turnaround companies)* (`[0:41:57]`)
- `Market Capitalization > ₹1,000 crore` *(can lower to ₹500 cr or ₹100 cr, though sub-₹100 cr names often carry negative cash flows or weak models)* (`[0:42:15]`)
- **Valuation Filter:** `P/E < 40` (tightens output to **63 names**), with the **15–30x P/E** (or **15–35x**) range acting as the sweet spot where P/E rerating remains on the table (`[0:33:15–0:36:50, 0:42:15–0:42:52]`). Expecting 5x P/E growth stocks is unrealistic in a strong market (`[0:33:15–0:33:51]`).
#### Post-Screen Research Workflow & Exceptions
Screening is strictly step one—**a probabilistic curiosity filter, not a buy signal** (`[0:30:38, 0:32:14–0:33:15, 0:44:12]`). Once a company surfaces (especially after multi-quarter stagnancy, e.g., Garware Technical Fibres posting 28% revenue growth), inspect press releases, investor presentations, concalls, DRHPs (for recent IPOs), and YouTube management interviews to verify whether the trigger is **cyclical or structural** (`[0:28:12–0:32:50]`).

Key exceptions where raw quarterly P&L screens mislead (`[0:26:43–0:28:12, 0:40:00–0:41:18]`):

- **Deferred Revenue & Churn (IndiaMART):** Despite ~100% PAT growth, IndiaMART fell ~20% (₹3,000 → ₹2,400) because markets discounted slowing subscriber additions and rising churn (`[0:26:43–0:28:12]`).
- **Real Estate Accounting (Arvind SmartSpaces):** Revenue/PAT on the P&L lags actual business momentum; investors must track **pre-sales** (e.g., ₹2,260 cr business vs. ₹11 cr reported quarterly PAT) (`[0:40:00–0:40:43]`).
- **AUM-Linked vs. Volume Growth (UTI AMC):** At ~20x P/E, verify whether AMC earnings growth comes from net inflows/volume or merely rising equity market levels (`[0:34:37–0:34:58]`).
### c) Examples & case studies
- **Example:** IEX, ZenTech, Cummins India, Aditya Birla AMC, Tinna Rubber, Shakti Pumps (`[0:24:21–0:25:40, 0:42:52–0:43:27]`)
  - Data/details: IEX (+28% growth), ZenTech (+360% PAT), Cummins India (+37% PAT), AB AMC (+36% PAT), Tinna Rubber (+60% PAT + capacity expansion), Shakti Pumps (+1,600% PAT across 3 quarters, surfaced directly on Screener).
- **Example:** Varun Beverages & Jash Engineering (`[0:25:40–0:26:43, 0:30:38–0:31:31]`)
  - Data/details: Varun Beverages sustained screen qualification for 12 quarters; Jash Engineering qualified for 6 quarters after emerging from a lull.
- **Example:** Garware Technical Fibres & Signpost India (`[0:28:12–0:32:14]`)
  - Data/details: Garware Technical Fibres broke multi-quarter stagnancy with 28% revenue growth (Nov 14 press release: 16% net sales, 30% PAT growth driven by aquaculture and geosynthetics). Signpost India showed top-line explosion after 2–3 quarters.
- **Example:** Pricol, Pearl Global, Pokarna, Ritco Logistics, Sandur Manganese, J Kumar Infra, Sarda Energy (`[0:28:12, 0:34:58–0:45:23]`)
  - Data/details: Pricol acquired Sundaram/Shriram [?] adding ₹750 cr sales; Pokarna posted 2 quarters of 19–25% growth; Ritco Logistics showed 3-year PAT growth of 78% and TTM 35% + Monarch Capital coverage; Sandur Manganese posted 40–65% YoY growth post-mining/capex commercialization; J Kumar Infra posted 17% sales growth and PAT ₹73 cr → ₹90 cr (+22–23%, margins 14% → 15%); Sarda Energy acquired SKS power plant (guided ~₹2,500 cr EBITDA potential per Digant Haria).

## FAS-Lec04 – Stage 4: Combining CANSLIM "C, A, N" with Concalls & Sector Tailwinds [0:45:23–0:53:34] [TVGP: Theme, Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
How do you pair CANSLIM's "C" and "A" quantitative growth filters with "N" (New products, acquisitions, or policies) in conference calls and investor presentations, and why does investor temperament shape screening success?

#### Debunking the Delivery Percentage Myth
Many investors dismiss high-volume breakouts because the NSE/BSE delivery percentage drops on breakout days [0:57:30–0:59:14]:

- **Normal Day:** 1,000 shares traded (`[? verify: STT says "100 shares" at 0:57:30]`) at **50% delivery** = **500 shares** delivered into demat accounts [0:57:30].
- **Catalyst/News Day:** Following a major trigger (e.g., anti-dumping duty), **10,000 shares** trade at **20% delivery** = **2,000 shares** delivered into demat accounts [0:58:09].
- **Rule:** Even though delivery *percentage* fell from 50% to 20%, **absolute delivery quantity quadrupled** (2,000 vs. 500 shares) [0:58:09–0:58:56]. In ~99% of high-volume spikes, absolute delivery is much higher than normal; keep it simple and track price-volume action rather than delivery % [0:58:56–0:59:14].

#### 2. Stan Weinstein's Stage Analysis: The Mountain-Climb Analogy [1:36:56–1:42:19] [Slides 25–28]
Using a mountain-climb drawing, the speaker maps the energy required to climb vs. descend onto the four stages of a stock [1:36:56–1:39:27]:

- **Stage 1 (Basing Stage):** Walking on a flat road—value stocks move sideways in a long horizontal base, oscillating above and below the **30-week moving average** while ownership transfers from weak hands to strong hands and valuation becomes cheap [1:37:47–1:39:57; Slide 25].
- **Stage 2 (Advancing Stage):** Climbing uphill—takes the most energy and begins when a **positive earnings surprise** removes stale market expectations via one of the 7–8 Variant Perception triggers [1:38:51–1:40:39; Slide 26]. - **Stage 3 (Consolidation / Topping):** Reaching the top—growth expectations become overly optimistic, the 30-week moving average flattens, and price oscillates sideways [1:41:14–1:41:47; Slide 27].
- **Stage 4 (Breakdown / Downtrends):** Descending downhill—when the earnings growth cycle ends and EPS momentum is lost, negative surprises trigger estimate cuts and a brutal fall below the 200 DMA/EMA until everyone sells out and the stock eventually returns to Stage 1 value territory [1:41:14–1:41:47; Slide 28].

To master Stage Analysis, an investor must conquer four elements [1:41:47–1:42:19]:

- **Relative Strength (RS)**
- **Fundamental catalyst**
- **30-week EMA** (distinguishing sideways, upward, and downward structures)
- **Price-volume action** (price up + volume up = strong uptrend; price up + volume down = weakening uptrend)
#### 3. Price-Volume Action: The Shimla Mall Road Restaurant Analogy [1:42:19–1:44:56]
To make price-volume behavior unforgettable, the speaker uses a restaurant analogy set on Shimla's Mall Road [1:42:19–1:44:14]:

#### 4. Relative Strength (RS) vs. RSI, Academic Proof & Sports Analogy [1:44:56–1:47:56] [Slides 18–21, 30–31]
- **RS vs. RSI:** **Relative Strength (RS)** compares a stock's performance against an index/benchmark (`Price of XYZ ÷ Price of Market Average` per Stan Weinstein), whereas **RSI** is a momentum oscillator—never confuse the two [1:44:56–1:46:42]. If Stock XYZ rises 10% while the index rises 20%, XYZ has *poor* RS and is being pulled reluctantly higher; if XYZ falls 10% while the market plunges 20%, XYZ shows *positive* RS and will likely lead when the market turns up [1:45:28–1:46:42].

## 7. GLOSSARY
- **B2B / B2G (Business-to-Business / Business-to-Government):** Business models selling to other corporates or government tenders; favored over saturated B2C staples due to multi-year order books and capex cycles [0:12:55–0:16:34].
- **Blue Dot List (MarketSmith India):** A screen flagging stocks whose Relative Strength (RS) line hits a 52-week high, even before the stock price itself reaches a 52-week high [1:10:48–1:12:11].
- **Bullwhip Effect (Stocking & De-stocking):** Multi-year cycle where downstream buyers over-order during strong demand to avoid stockouts, followed by inventory de-stocking lulls [0:13:47–0:15:22].
- **CANSLIM:** William O'Neil's 7-factor growth-investing framework; lecture highlights **C** (Current Big/Accelerating Quarterly Earnings & Sales), **A** (Annual Earnings), **N** (New products/management/highs), **S** (Supply & Demand), **L** (Leader vs. Laggard), and **I** (Institutional Sponsorship) [0:08:59–0:09:35, 0:45:23, 1:24:03] [Slides 6–11].
- **CWIP (Capital Work in Progress):** Balance sheet line item indicating ongoing capex not yet commercialized (e.g., ₹52 cr CWIP in Ice Make Refrigeration) [2:00:37–2:02:13].
- **Delivery Percentage:** Percentage of total traded shares settled into demat accounts; often drops on high-volume news/breakout days even as absolute delivered shares surge [0:57:30–0:59:14].
- **GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price):** Investing in companies with strong earnings growth at reasonable multiples (e.g., 15–30x P/E) so both earnings and P/E rerating drive returns [0:17:37, 0:33:15–0:36:50].
- **LCA (Laggard-to-Leader / MarketSmith Post-Correction Screen):** MarketSmith India screen used after market corrections to spot emerging sector leaders transitioning from laggard to leader [1:05:02, 1:05:56].
- **Ovarian Lottery:** Warren Buffett's analogy referenced to note how investor temperament (curiosity vs. cynicism) shapes idea discovery [0:51:33–0:52:17].
- **PE Overhang / Oversupply:** Price stagnation caused by pre-IPO private equity or institutional investors steadily selling into retail hands despite cheap EV/EBITDA multiples (e.g., Samhi Hotels) [1:24:43–1:26:50].
- **Relative Strength (RS) vs. RSI:** **RS** compares a stock's price performance against an index/benchmark (Price of XYZ ÷ Price of Market Average per Stan Weinstein), whereas **RSI** is an internal momentum oscillator [1:12:49, 1:44:56–1:46:42].
- **Stage Analysis (Stages 1–4):** Stan Weinstein's market cycle model: Stage 1 (Basing around 30W EMA), Stage 2 (Advancing uphill on earnings surprise + RS), Stage 3 (Consolidation/topping as 30W EMA flattens), and Stage 4 (Breakdown below 200 DMA/EMA) [1:36:56–1:42:19] [Slides 25–28].
- **Time Correction:** Prolonged sideways price action during earnings/margin contraction that quietly inflates a stock's P/E multiple and erodes opportunity cost (e.g., SRF) [1:49:21–1:52:44].
- **Variant Perception:** Fundamental change triggers (margin expansion, demergers, debt reduction, product mix shift, capex, operating leverage, management change, industry turnaround) that drive a stock from Stage 1 into Stage 2 [1:36:04–1:36:56].
- **Volume Rocketing:** StockScans screen identifying stocks where 10-day average volume is ≥ 1.5x the 20-day average volume (or 5-day vs. 10-day) [1:01:08].

## BS-Lec10 – Stage 1: Webinar Context & Overview of Core Buying Approaches  [00:00:06–00:11:31]  [TVGP: Theme / Valuation / Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
What is the scope of this two-part "When to Buy a Stock" series, and what are the major fundamental and continuation-based approaches investors use to enter a stock?
### b) Detailed explanation
**Two-part series scope & disclaimer:** This session is Part 1 of a two-part framework series focused on **when and how to buy a stock** (Part 2 covers how to hold and when/how to sell) [00:00:06–00:00:37]. Standard SEBI-style disclaimer: nothing presented is a buy or sell recommendation; case studies are strictly educational [00:00:37]. The goal is to ensure that whenever an investor buys a stock, they have complete clarity on *which* investing style and buying framework they are applying (e.g., the speaker personally mixes GARP and continuation buying while tracking the exact rationale for each position) [00:01:10–00:02:07].

**Six broader fundamental buying approaches:**

- **Value approach [TVGP: Valuation]:** Targets businesses trading below `1x` Price-to-Book (`P/B < 1`), at lifetime-low multiples, with very high dividend yields (e.g., `7%–12%`), or where last year's margins were crushed to the bottom quartile and ROE/ROA can inflect [00:02:07–00:03:09].
- **Momentum approach [TVGP: Growth]:** Ignores starting valuation and follows the strongest EPS/earnings growth paired with **continuation buying patterns** on charts (where both primary and secondary trends are positive and the stock breaks out of a consolidation) [00:03:17–00:03:50, 00:10:07–00:11:00]. Fast-growing mid/large caps often attract mutual fund herding and reach high P/E multiples [00:10:07–00:10:37].
- **Pessimism approach:** Similar to value, but triggered by a **one-off event** that causes a sharp stock-price crash even though the underlying franchise and balance sheet remain intact and will survive [00:03:50–00:05:30].
- **Special situation approach:** Event-driven corporate changes such as management changes, private equity entry, demergers ("parent letting go of the child"), reverse mergers, deleveraging, or **self-help stories** (reducing capital allocation to bad businesses and shifting it to good businesses) [00:05:30–00:06:40, 00:11:00].
- **Fundamental trigger approach [TVGP: Growth]:** Inflections driven by rising capacity utilization, new Capex, geographical expansion, **inorganic acquisitions**, or operating leverage [00:06:40–00:08:09, 00:11:31].
- **Pre-Opex & Bottom Cycle / Bottom Margins:**
  - *Bottom cycle / bottom margins:* Linked to value/pessimism when external macro factors (e.g., elections, heat waves halting infrastructure Capex) push margins to multi-year lows [00:08:09–00:08:50].
  - *Pre-Opex mental model:* When a company commissions new Capex, day-one utilization is low because product sampling and customer approvals take time, while employee and power costs hit the P&L immediately—depressing near-term earnings before utilization ramps up [00:08:50–00:10:07].

## BS-Lec10 – Stage 2: Unique Buying Patterns & Technical Continuation vs. Reversal Schools  [00:11:31–00:20:32]  [TVGP: Valuation / Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
What are SOIC's unique hybrid buying patterns (Bombed-Out IPOs, Speed Breaker vs. Valley, Convexity), and how do technical continuation, reversal, and techno-funda (CANSLIM) schools differ?
### b) Detailed explanation
**1. Bombed-Out IPOs:** Pre-IPO numbers are typically window-dressed/inflated because selling promoters want high valuations (incentive-caused bias) [00:11:31–00:12:45]. Post-IPO, 3–4 quarters of struggle against that elevated base trigger a series of lower highs and lower lows [00:12:45–00:13:33]. After **1.5–2 years** (bottoming process often 9–12 months), the base normalizes, management adapts to public markets, and the stock enters a sideways "ignorance" phase where investors abuse it [00:12:45–00:13:46]. When earnings surprises return, 30-weekly EMA turns up, higher highs/higher lows begin, and an **IPO-base breakout** occurs [00:13:46–00:14:50]. Value investors buy the sideways base; GARP investors buy the breakout trigger [00:14:50].

**2. Speed Breaker vs. Valley ("Khai"):** In the speed breaker (pessimism) approach, a fast-moving stock hits a one-off event [00:16:18]. The investor must distinguish a temporary **speed breaker** from a permanent **valley (`khai`)** where the business model breaks [00:16:18–00:17:24].

**3. Convexity Approach:** A company doing steady manufacturing announces a multi-fold capacity expansion or massive order that transforms its future size and scale relative to Capex [00:17:24–00:17:45].

**4. Technical School — Continuation vs. Reversal:**

- **Continuation buying:** Buying horizontal-line or sloping-line breakouts (cup, ascending triangle, inverse head-and-shoulders, or **Nicolas Darvas Box**—sideways consolidation between support and resistance inside an existing uptrend) [00:17:45–00:19:09].
- **Reversal buying:** Buying after selling exhaustion marked by a high-volume reversal candle and rounding bottom [00:19:09–00:20:02].

**5. Techno-Funda & CANSLIM:** Combines earnings growth with chart confirmation; pure technical/techno-funda investors treat **market environment as 60%–70% of the game** and sit in cash during unfavorable regimes [00:20:02–00:20:32]. William O'Neil's **CANSLIM** (*How to Make Money in Stocks*) integrates new products, new management, and market environment [00:20:32].
### c) Examples & case studies
- **Bombed-Out IPOs:** *CarTrade*, *Zomato*, *PB FinTech*, and *Supriya Lifescience* all went through post-IPO sell-offs followed by flat rounding-bottom bases and multi-year breakouts [00:15:23–00:15:54].
- **Valley (`Khai`) vs. Speed Breaker:** *Delta Corp* received a `₹32,000 crore` GST notice against a fraction of that market cap—a structural valley (`khai`), not a temporary speed breaker [00:16:18–00:16:54].
- **Reversal Candle:** *Sudarshan Chemical* (18–24 months prior) showed selling exhaustion on a high-volume reversal candle, followed 6–12 months later by the *Heubach* [?] ("high back chemicals") acquisition and business turnaround [00:19:09–00:20:02].

## BS-Lec10 – Stage 3: Four Investor Factors & The Six Blind Men Parable  [00:20:32–00:32:05]
### a) What this stage answers
What four personal and market factors determine which buying framework fits you, and why does combining multiple lenses ("Six Blind Men and the Elephant") outperform dogmatic single-style investing?
### b) Detailed explanation
**Four factors governing your buying approach:**

- **Time Horizon:** Positional trading (`6–12 months`), GARP (`18–36 months`), Value investing (`2–3 years`), or Intraday (not covered) [00:21:03–00:21:35].
- **Expectations:** Investors who cannot tolerate 1–2 months of red on their portfolio need short-term technicals or cash calls; those aiming to beat the index by `5%–10%` over 3–4 years suit longer-term fundamental frameworks [00:21:35–00:22:10].
- **Market Environment:** Even in a 2.5–3 year bear market, selective pockets (e.g., Railways via budgetary allocations, or PVR post-2021) thrive, while weak environments crush post-QIP stocks with deteriorating return ratios by ~60% [00:22:10–00:23:58]. At absurd index valuations (e.g., `40–50x` P/E vs. `21–21.5x` average), value investors hold cash; below `20x` index P/E is a strong lump-sum zone for index/mutual fund investors [00:23:58–00:25:27].
- **Personal Persona:** "Personal finance is personal"—patience, temperament, and available time dictate whether you belong in index funds, technical stop-loss trading, or multi-year fundamental triggers [00:24:57–00:27:20].

**The Six Blind Men & The Elephant:** Just as six blind men touch different parts of an elephant (tummy = wall, tail = rope, leg = tree, trunk = snake, tusk = spear, ear = fan), each investing school captures one truth [00:27:20–00:28:00]:

- **Value buying** (leg/tree): Balance sheet, intrinsic value, margin of safety; misses momentum/narratives [00:28:00–00:28:57].
- **Technicals** (tail/rope): Price patterns, moving averages, volumes; captures psychology but misses fundamentals [00:28:00–00:28:57].
- **Techno-funda** (trunk + tusk): Growth numbers + chart confirmation; fuller view, though choppy markets still cause whipsaws [00:28:57–00:29:25].
- **Momentum** (ear/fan): Trend strength and flows; ignores valuations and fails on sudden reversals [00:28:57–00:29:25].
- **CANSLIM / GARP:** Hybrid growth at reasonable prices (the speaker's own blend of value + CANSLIM) [00:29:25–00:30:15].

No single approach is superior—match your core method to your temperament, but understand other lenses so you avoid traps (e.g., using fundamentals to reject shady technical charts) [00:30:48–00:32:05].
### c) Examples & case studies
- **QIP Recycling Company:** Raised a QIP 18 months prior; return ratios deteriorated, 2–3 quarters of growth stalled, and valuations collapsed `~60%` to `15–20x` P/E in a weak market—attracting value investors while momentum investors waited for new higher highs [00:22:31–00:23:29].
- **Brightcom Group ("Brightcom" / shady IT companies):** Pure technicals might trigger a buy, but basic fundamental checks immediately reveal governance/business red flags [00:31:13–00:31:39].

## BS-Lec10 – Stage 4: Bombed-Out IPOs, Upgrade/Downgrade Cycles & Inflection Screening  [00:32:05–00:46:17]  [TVGP: Growth / Valuation]
### a) What this stage answers
How do you execute the Bombed-Out IPO and Rounding Bottom frameworks using 30-weekly EMA and earnings inflection, and which StockScans trackers surface inflection candidates?
### b) Detailed explanation
**Bombed-Out IPO execution rules (3 entry points):**

- **Trend change:** Buy when lower highs stop forming, higher highs/higher lows begin, and the **30-weekly EMA** turns upward [00:32:33–00:34:18, 00:37:19–00:38:41].
- **IPO-base breakout:** Buy when the stock breaks out above its post-IPO horizontal base [00:32:33–00:33:33].
- **Improved results / guidance:** Fundamental entry when margins, PAT, or forward guidance inflect upward after the benign base forms (typically **15–24 months / 1.5–2 years** post-IPO) [00:32:33–00:33:33, 00:41:35–00:42:45].

**Upgrade vs. Downgrade Cycles:** Positive earnings surprises kick off an **upgrade cycle** as analysts raise estimates [00:38:41–00:38:51]. Conversely, an **earnings downgrade cycle** (e.g., from Chinese chemical dumping) can persist for **8–12 quarters** or even 4 years, keeping Relative Strength (`RS`) negative [00:38:51–00:40:36].

**Inflection & Market-Share Gains:** Companies gaining market share against incumbents—alongside improving capacity utilization post-Capex, order-book build, margin expansion, strong export data (tracked via Sekhar's `Learning 11` Telegram channel), and massive breakout volumes—drive durable upgrade cycles [00:42:45–00:44:57].

**StockScans Inflection Screens:**

- **Volume Rocketing:** Flags massive volume surges signaling smart-money entry [00:44:57–00:45:20].
- **Long-Term Breakouts:** Surfaces stocks emerging from multi-year consolidations [00:45:20].
- **Explosive Growth / Buoyant Growth [?] ("boy and growth"):** Fundamental growth screens [00:45:20].
- **Strong Quarterly Numbers / Upcoming Results:** Screens companies reporting **`> 25%` quarterly growth** [00:45:47–00:46:17].
### c) Examples & case studies
- **PB FinTech:** Lower highs stopped, management guided for `₹1,000 crore` [? verify: transcript says `8000 crore pad` / `1000`?] PAT, 30-weekly EMA turned up, and results accelerated [00:34:18].
- **Delhivery:** Reported 4–5 quarters of post-listing losses before swinging to its first profitable quarter and improving results [00:34:51–00:35:15].

# BS-Lec10 – Stage 5: Speed Breaker Approach: Temporary Overhangs vs. Permanent Structural Breaks  [00:46:17–01:00:35]  [TVGP: Valuation / Theme]
## a) What this stage answers
How can an investor use first-principles thinking to separate temporary "speed breaker" panics from permanent structural breaks ("valleys"), and manage uncertainty through portfolio sizing?
## b) Detailed explanation
Building on the bombed-out IPO and inflection frameworks (see Stage 4), the Speed Breaker Approach targets sharp stock-price free falls triggered by sudden uncertain news—such as **GST notices, resolvable regulatory issues, key-person resignations, COVID-style shocks, tariff uncertainties, or noise events** [00:46:17–00:47:04].

**1. The Core Test — Temporary Speed Breaker vs. Permanent Structural Break:**
The critical skill is cutting through market panic and thinking from **first principles** to judge whether the shock is short-lived or permanently damages the business model [00:46:40–00:47:04, 00:59:40–01:00:35]:

- **Tax/GST Notices:** In India, almost every company carries routine tax disputes in its annual report contingent liabilities going back to the 1990s [00:47:32–00:47:55]. If a ₹1,000 Cr GST notice hits a ₹30,000 Cr market-cap company with ₹600 Cr cash and strong cash flows, it is typically a temporary overhang ("will not kill the business") [00:47:04–00:47:55]. By contrast, a ₹32,000 Cr GST notice on a ₹4,000 Cr market-cap casino business is existential/permanent [00:49:30–00:50:01].
- **Management & Auditor Exits:** A single CEO resignation over an acquisition dispute (e.g., Vishal Sikka at Infosys) can be a temporary speed breaker [00:57:59–00:58:34], whereas **3 CFO resignations in a year across back-to-back quarters plus an auditor resignation** (like the 2017–18 wave or Gensol Engineering [?]) signals governance rot ("something could be fishy") and must be avoided [00:47:55–00:49:05].
- **Regulatory & Tariff Shocks:** Distinguish habit-driven demand (e.g., a 10%–15% Maharashtra liquor tax hike, raising a ₹600 bottle to ₹660–₹700, does not stop habitual consumption) or temporary US tariff threats from permanent regulatory re-architecting (e.g., **market coupling** turning an electricity exchange into a mere order-routing broker) [00:50:01–00:52:51].
- **Weather / Seasonal Delays:** Extended monsoons hurting AC sales in an under-penetrated market like India are temporary unless abnormal rains repeat every year [00:48:22–00:49:30].

**2. Risk Management via 2%–4%–6%–8% Allocation & First Principles:**
Good news and good prices rarely arrive together—some tariff-hit stocks fell to **12–16x P/E** before management clarified the impact and re-rated [00:53:22–00:54:09]. Because uncertainty makes sitting through panics difficult (and a temporary issue can turn permanent), manage risk at the portfolio level using **2%–4%–6%–8% staged allocation** [00:52:51–00:54:09]. When the temporary speed breaker resolves, the "car" accelerates back to 100 km/h—delivering exceptionally high near-term IRR [00:54:09–00:55:11, 00:59:40–01:00:16].

## BS-Lec10 – Stage 9: Pessimism Plays: Sectoral Downcycles, Temp Shocks & Mean Reversion  [01:30:03–01:36:36]  [TVGP: Valuation]

**Characteristics of Pessimism Plays:** Unlike special situations (which have explicit corporate catalysts like demergers or buybacks), **in pessimism plays, "only time can heal the wound"** [01:35:45–01:36:21]. They feature:

## 7. GLOSSARY
- **30-Weekly EMA / 21-Weekly EMA:** Weekly exponential moving averages used to confirm Stage 2 trend turns (Bombed-Out IPOs) and enforce trailing exits when momentum/hyper-growth slows [00:13:46, 01:03:57–01:04:13].
- **Blue Dot List (MarketSmith):** Screen highlighting stocks whose Relative Strength (RS) hits a 52-week high while breaking out of a base pattern [01:03:20–01:04:13].
- **Bombed-Out IPO:** Newly listed stock whose pre-IPO window-dressed numbers lead to 3–4 quarters of post-IPO de-growth, followed by 15–24 months of base building before an earnings surprise and 30W EMA turnaround [00:11:31–00:15:54, 00:32:05–00:42:45].
- **CANSLIM:** William O'Neil's techno-funda framework (*How to Make Money in Stocks*) combining earnings growth, new products/management, and market environment [00:20:32].
- **Continuation Buying:** Entering a stock with positive primary and secondary trends as it breaks out of a consolidation pattern (cup, ascending triangle, inverse head-and-shoulders, or Nicolas Darvas Box) [00:17:45–00:19:09].
- **Convexity Approach:** Investing in steady manufacturing companies announcing multi-fold capacity expansions or transformative orders relative to Capex [00:17:24–00:17:45].
- **Demerger:** Corporate restructuring ("parent letting go of the child") separating distinct businesses to unlock conglomerate/holding-company discounts [00:05:30, 01:07:16–01:07:52].
- **EPS Rating (MarketSmith):** Percentile ranking of a company's earnings growth against all listed stocks (e.g., 93 = top 7%) [01:02:27–01:03:20].
- **GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price):** Hybrid investing style blending fundamental growth triggers with reasonable valuation (18–36 month horizon) [00:01:35, 00:21:03, 00:29:25].
- **Market Coupling:** Regulatory mechanism turning electricity exchanges into mere order-collecting/bid-routing brokers, cited as a permanent structural break for IEX [00:50:30–00:51:30].
- **Nicolas Darvas Box:** Continuation pattern where price consolidates sideways between horizontal support and resistance inside an existing uptrend before breaking out [00:18:15–00:19:09].
- **Pessimism Play:** Deep-value contrarian setup in a hated sector at Stage 4 lows after 4–8 quarters of downgrades where "only time heals the wound" [01:30:03–01:36:36].
- **Pre-Opex:** Mental model where new Capex immediately adds employee and power costs to the P&L before customer approvals and capacity utilization ramp up [00:08:50–00:10:07].
- **Purple Patch:** Period of ferocious quarterly PAT/EBITDA acceleration paired with Stage 2 technicals in Fundamental Momentum Investing [01:01:04–01:02:27].
- **Reversal Buying:** Technical entry after selling exhaustion marked by a high-volume reversal candle and rounding bottom [00:19:09–00:20:02].
- **Reverse Merger:** Merging a subsidiary/NBFC into its listed parent to eliminate holding-company discounts and simplify ownership [01:08:27–01:09:37].
- **Self-Help Story:** Company actively improving its own capital allocation—refinancing high-cost debt, shutting loss-making units, fixing subsidiaries, or shifting from wholesale to B2C—without waiting for macro tailwinds [01:22:47–01:28:39].
- **Speed Breaker vs. Valley (*Khai*):** Distinguishing a temporary, resolvable panic (e.g., Polycab IT raid, MCX software migration) from a permanent structural break (e.g., Delta Corp ₹32,000 Cr GST notice, IEX market coupling) [00:16:18–00:17:24, 00:46:40–01:00:35].
- **Time Stop-Loss:** Mandatory exit rule (4–8 quarters) if a value thesis fails to play out, preventing multi-decade value traps and opportunity cost [01:52:59–01:53:49].
- **Volume Rocketing (StockScans):** Screen flagging abnormal volume surges that signal smart-money accumulation [00:44:57–00:45:20, 01:48:01–01:49:18].

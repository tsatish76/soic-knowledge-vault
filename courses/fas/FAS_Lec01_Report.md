## 2. KEY LEARNINGS
- **Return Equation & Market Perception:** Stock returns are driven by three factors: (1) market perception of the theme/stock, (2) earnings growth, and (3) the rate of change of perception about earnings growth (`Perception / Earnings = P/E`) [0:09:18].
- **Listen to the Market (70–30 / 80–20 Rule):** Terry Smith's Fundsmith shrank from £23B to £12B with portfolio turnover spiking from ~8% to 51% after underperforming for 3–5 years; Speaker's view: allocate 70–80% of a 10-stock portfolio (6–7 stocks) with prevailing industry trends and reserve 20–30% (3–4 stocks) for contrarian bets [0:01:36, 0:05:02].
- **Fundamental Analysis ≠ Accounting:** Fundamental analysis is a probabilistic study of business rate-of-change and future trajectory, not backward-looking accounting or 300-page forensics; drop the trailing-12-month (TTM) P/E "warrior" mindset in favor of a J-curve mindset [0:14:39, 0:23:18, 0:41:47].
- **The "NEW" Catalyst Framework:** Every major wealth creator is driven by something *new*—new base creation, industry cycle, management/promoter change, corporate action, capex, new-age business, new market/geography, regulations, value-added products, verticals, acquisitions, warrant issues, or balance sheet deleveraging [0:15:55, 1:25:52].
- **S-Curve Inflection & Theme Expiry:** New technology adoption follows an S-curve (e.g., electric 2-wheelers crossing 10% registration share per Rohit Chauhan; EV 3-wheelers reaching 60% share), but every theme carries an expiry date once adoption saturates [0:26:33, 0:43:12, 0:43:46].
- **Warrant Issues as a Bullish Signal:** A 6-year backtest across 686 companies (>₹500 cr market cap) issuing promoter warrants (25% upfront capital, 18-month conversion lock-in) showed a 68% hit rate over 2 years (145% avg return), 72% over 3 years, and 78% over 5 years (459% avg / 105% median return) [0:48:27, 0:50:35].
- **Value-Chain Climbing:** Moving up the value chain into value-added products (e.g., KSH International HVDC 400kV CTC cables lifting EBITDA/ton from ₹59,000 to ₹74,000; premium liquor mix) expands gross, EBITDA, and PAT margins [0:54:01, 0:59:00, 1:00:16].
- **Scan Match & Announcement Automation:** Combining fundamental scans (Buoyant Growth: >20% sales & PAT growth; Explosive Growth: >20% sales; Margin Mavericks; Driving Deleverage) with technical breakout scans via **Scan Match**, paired with real-time StockScans corporate announcement & annual report red-flag alerts (Qualified/Disclaimer of Opinion, SEBI notices), systematizes idea discovery [1:08:07, 1:09:30, 1:21:51].
- **2-4-6-8 Portfolio Sizing:** Maintain 20–25 stocks using conviction-weighted position sizing: 2% (low), 4% (OK), 6% (good), and 8% (very high conviction), with most positions clustered at 4–6% [1:27:48].

## FAS-Lec01 – Stage 1: Why Growth Catalysts Matter: The Terry Smith Case & Market Reality  [0:00:06–0:08:41]  [TVGP: Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
Why does pure "buy quality and do nothing" investing underperform in momentum-driven markets, and why must investors align 70–80% of their portfolio with prevailing fundamental and industry growth catalysts?
### b) Detailed explanation
#### The Genesis of the Growth Catalyst Course: Terry Smith's Letter
The session opens by examining why studying growth catalysts is essential for active equity investors [0:00:06–0:00:36]. Historically, "quality investing" (often framed as growth at any price for high-quality businesses) was popularized globally by Terry Smith of Fundsmith, whose mantra was widely cited in India during 2017–2019—buying "coffee can" style quality businesses without worrying about valuation [0:00:36–0:01:36].

However, Terry Smith's recent shareholder letter highlights a sharp reckoning [0:01:36–0:02:11]:

- **Performance & AUM Erosion:** Fundsmith lost money over the prior 12 months, while 3-year and 5-year returns fell to roughly a third of peers [0:01:36]. Assets under management (AUM) shrank by nearly 40–50%, dropping from £23 billion three years ago to £12 billion [0:01:36–0:02:11].
- **Broken "Do Nothing" Mantra:** Fundsmith's original three-step mantra was: (1) buy good companies, (2) don't overpay, and (3) do nothing [0:02:11]. Historically, portfolio turnover sat in single digits (~8%, implying a 12.5-year average holding period) [0:02:40]. In the first half of the recent year, turnover spiked to 51% as Smith was forced to make significant portfolio changes [0:02:11–0:02:40].
- **Closed vs. Open-Ended Reality:** Smith noted that in a market where even large-cap stocks move 33% in a single day, pure buy-and-hold only works if a manager is insulated from fund flows—noting that Warren Buffett executed his strategy in a closed vehicle he controlled rather than an open-ended fund [0:03:19, 0:06:39].
#### Listening to the Market vs. Fighting the Tide
Terry Smith remarked that sticking to an unchanged approach risks falling foul of the adage that *"the market can remain illogical longer than we can remain in business"* (echoing *"the market can remain irrational longer than we can remain solvent"*) [0:03:19–0:03:50]. Speaker's view: if the market points in one direction for 3 to 5 years—a substantial horizon—it is worth asking whether the market is illogical or whether the investor's own actions are out of step with reality [0:03:50].

The market is treated as the "supreme being" [0:03:50–0:04:15]. Across the last 5–7 years, big investing personalities and retail investors who followed them blindly suffered for years when fighting prevailing trends, whereas retail investors who grew into HNIs and UHNIs did so by listening to the market and swimming *with* the tide rather than against it [0:04:15–0:05:02].
#### The 70–30 / 80–20 Portfolio Split & Catching Falling Knives
Speaker's view on balancing trend-following with contrarian investing (a Pareto-style split) [0:05:02–0:05:34]:

- **70–80% With the Trend:** In a 10-stock portfolio, 6–7 stocks should align with prevailing industry trends and active growth catalysts [0:05:02].
- **20–30% Contrarian:** Reserve 20–30% (3–4 stocks in a 10-stock portfolio) for contrarian bets where you hold a fundamental view for 2–3 years before the tide arrives [0:05:02].
- **Avoid Catching Falling Knives:** Terry Smith explicitly acknowledged taking greater account of both fundamental and share-price momentum, noting that buying quality companies "when they hit a glitch" (such as buying Microsoft at the end of Steve Ballmer's tenure) in a momentum-driven market is like catching a falling knife—leaving an investor with "cut fingers" as downward spirals are exacerbated by index momentum [0:05:34–0:06:39]. While share-price momentum is covered in Level 4 (Stage Analysis), this course focuses on **fundamental business momentum**—identifying companies delivering abnormal growth over two years and beyond [0:05:34–0:06:08].

## FAS-Lec01 – Stage 2: The Return Equation & Mindset Shift (PE Warrior vs. J-Curve)  [0:08:41–0:15:39]  [TVGP: Theme, Valuation, Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
What mathematical equation governs stock returns, and why must investors shift from a backward-looking "accounting/forensics" mindset to a probabilistic, forward-looking business mindset?
### b) Detailed explanation
#### The Stock Return Equation
Markets are slaves to earnings, and market perception is a slave to thematic tailwinds [0:08:41]. Perception is rarely static—it continuously moves either positively or negatively [0:08:41]. Defining valuation through perception:

$$\text{P/E Ratio} = \frac{\text{Perception}}{\text{Earnings}}$$

Building from this, the speaker presents the core three-factor **Return Equation** [0:09:18–0:09:53]:

- **Perception of the theme and stock** (captured via technicals and sector-wide volume/flows) [0:09:18, 0:10:26].
- **Earnings Growth** of the company (the core focus of this class) [0:09:18, 0:10:26].
- **Rate of Change of Perception about Earnings Growth** (driven by identifiable growth catalysts that cause the market to re-rate future growth expectations) [0:09:18, 0:11:20].
#### Why Stocks Run Up Before Reported Numbers Improve
Retail investors frequently notice that stocks start rallying *before* reported quarterly numbers visibly improve, and mistakenly assume the market is rigged or driven by insider trading [0:11:20–0:11:57]. Speaker's view: this is rarely insider trading; rather, the company has usually already signaled upcoming growth triggers (through announcements, capacity additions, JVs, or commentary), and the gap lies in the investor's own framework [0:11:57–0:12:36]. Whenever a rate-of-change inflection occurs, there is almost always something **NEW** happening in the business or fundamentals [0:12:36].
#### Victim Mindset vs. Survivor Mindset
Investors must drop the "sorry retail investor" victim mindset—complaining that institutions have an unfair edge—and adopt a **survivor mindset** [0:13:08–0:13:37]. Other retail investors have cracked the market over the last 5–6 years by putting in focused effort and learning from first principles [0:13:37]. Stock names themselves are commodities; the true edge is a repeatable **framework** [0:13:37–0:14:08].
#### Fundamental Analysis ≠ Accounting or Forensics
Many technical traders dismiss fundamental analysis as mere "accounting" (reading balance sheets and income statements) [0:14:08–0:14:39]. Speaker's view: this is completely false [0:14:39].

- **Not an Accountant:** You do not become wealthy running tallies or writing 300-page forensic reports—very few pure forensic analysts become great investors [0:14:39–0:15:11].
- **Probabilistic Business Study:** Fundamental analysis is the probabilistic study of **rate of change**, understanding what **new** things are happening in a business, and estimating where that business will be in the future [0:14:39, 0:15:11–0:15:39].

In a promoter warrant issue, the promoter pays **25% (one-fourth) of the capital upfront** (speaker briefly said 10% then immediately self-corrected to 25% [0:50:10–0:50:35]), locks in today's share price, and has **18 months** to convert the warrants by paying the remaining 75% [0:50:10–0:50:35]. If the promoter refuses to convert, they **forfeit the 25% upfront money** [0:50:59]. Therefore, a promoter only issues warrants to themselves in **two scenarios** [0:50:10–0:50:35]:

- **Balance Sheet Repair:** The company's balance sheet is weak and needs equity infusion [0:50:35].
- **Upcoming J-Curve Inflection:** The promoter expects the business to enter a J-curve in growth and wants to lock in today's share price to capture bumper upside [0:50:35].

#### Climbing the Value Chain: Specialized Products & Margin Expansion

## 7. GLOSSARY
- **Behind-the-Meter (BTM) Power:** Generating electricity on-site at a data center via dedicated power plants (CCPP, fuel cells, or gas engines) to bypass 5-year utility grid connection delays [0:31:37].
- **Buoyant Growth (StockScans):** Pre-built fundamental scan filtering companies with both >20% sales growth and >20% PAT growth [1:08:39].
- **CCPP (Combined Cycle Power Plant):** A power plant where large natural gas turbines generate electricity and exhaust steam is recovered via HRSG to drive a secondary steam turbine [0:32:07].
- **CDMO / CRO:** Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization / Contract Research Organization (pharma outsourcing models, e.g., Sai Life Sciences shifting from 62% CDMO / 38% CRO to 70% / 30%) [0:17:04, 0:39:46] [Slide 31].
- **Conscientiousness:** Doing orderly, thorough work and continually seeking to climb the value chain, which naturally attracts opportunities and relationships [1:37:00].
- **CTC (Continuously Transposed Conductors):** Specialized high-value-added copper winding wires used in HVDC 400 kV / 765 kV transformers [0:54:01–0:56:03] [Slides 55–57].
- **DCP:** Discipline, Consistency, and Patience—core behavioral pillars of compounding [1:37:00–1:37:36].
- **Derivative Tailwind:** Secondary demand-displacement benefit created when price spikes or shortages in a primary supply chain (e.g., DDR5/NAND memory) push customers toward an alternative (e.g., refurbished IT hardware) [0:58:05].
- **Disclaimer of Opinion / Qualified Opinion / Key Audit Matter:** Auditor report disclosures indicating either inability to verify financial items (e.g., debt repayment ability or un-audited foreign subsidiaries) or areas of significant audit focus [1:21:51–1:22:56] [Slide 71].
- **Driving Deleverage (StockScans):** Fundamental scan filtering companies actively repaying debt and reducing interest costs to trigger valuation re-rating [1:08:07].
- **Explosive Growth (StockScans):** Fundamental scan filtering companies delivering >20% annual top-line (sales) growth [1:08:39].
- **Horizontal S-Curve:** Mapping component, chemical, and ecosystem suppliers across an emerging technology's value chain rather than focusing solely on headline OEMs [0:27:05–0:27:32].
- **HRSG (Heat Recovery Steam Generator):** Piping/boiler system that captures exhaust heat from gas turbines in a CCPP to produce steam [0:34:13].
- **J-Curve Mindset:** Forward-looking analytical mindset focused on exponential earnings inflections where prior top line converts into current bottom line, replacing trailing P/E obsession [0:20:33, 0:22:44–0:23:18].
- **LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme):** Used without definition (in context of 90% India / 10–15% global asset allocation limits) [1:35:21].
- **Margin Mavericks (StockScans):** Fundamental scan filtering companies whose operating/profit margins expanded year-over-year [1:08:39].
- **New Base Creation:** Step-change expansion in a company's revenue/EBITDA run-rate driven by transformative M&A, greenfield capex, or new product approvals [0:15:55, 1:01:00].
- **OSAT:** Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (referenced via CG Semi's G1 facility in Sanand) [Slide 19].
- **Pass-Through Denominator Effect:** Accounting optical effect where rising raw-material prices (e.g., copper) inflate revenue and depress percentage EBITDA margins even when absolute EBITDA per tonne rises [Slide 55].
- **PE Warrior (TTM P/E Warrior):** Investor fixated on backward-looking trailing-12-month P/E multiples who misses forward J-curve growth catalysts [0:22:44, 0:41:14, 1:29:28].
- **Price Anchoring:** Rejecting a multi-year growth stock because its price has already risen (e.g., from ₹2 to ₹10) rather than assessing its present 3–4 year forward trajectory [0:17:41–0:18:51].
- **QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement):** Equity capital raise from institutional investors to fund growth capex or balance sheet needs [Slides 8–9].
- **Relative Strength (RS):** Price outperformance of a stock or sector compared to the broader market index (like two wrestlers comparing strength) [1:06:27–1:06:41].
- **RPO (Remaining Performance Obligation):** Contracted order backlog metric used to track multi-quarter revenue visibility (e.g., GE Vernova) [Slide 25].
- **SAW / LSAW / HFIW / DI Pipes:** Submerged Arc Welded / Longitudinal SAW / High-Frequency Induction Welded / Ductile Iron pipes used in oil, gas, and water infrastructure [0:34:13, 0:36:26–0:38:10] [Slide 40].
- **Scan Match:** StockScans feature that intersects fundamental scans with technical breakout/volume scans and ranks stocks by total scan appearances [1:09:30] [Slide 69].
- **SOFC (Solid Oxide Fuel Cells):** Modular on-site power generation boxes offering faster time-to-power for data centers than grid connections [0:32:33].
- **Strong Quarterly Growth (StockScans):** Fundamental scan filtering companies with >20% YoY PAT growth in the latest quarter [1:05:50].
- **Theme Expiry Date:** Point at which an industry's S-curve adoption saturates or policy/structural headwinds erode terminal value [0:41:47–0:43:46].
- **Warrant Issue (Promoter):** Capital-raising instrument where promoters pay 25% upfront to lock in today's share price with an 18-month conversion window, forfeiting the 25% if unexercised [0:50:10–0:50:59].

## 11. SELF-TEST
- **Q:** What are the three components of the Stock Return Equation, and how is P/E defined behaviorally? **A:** Returns = Perception of theme/stock × Earnings Growth × Rate of Change of Perception about earnings growth; P/E = Perception ÷ Earnings (Stage 2).
- **Q:** According to the speaker's 70–30 / 80–20 rule, how should a 10-stock portfolio be divided between trend-aligned and contrarian bets? **A:** 70–80% (6–7 stocks) aligned with prevailing industry trends and growth catalysts, and 20–30% (3–4 stocks) in contrarian bets (Stage 1).
- **Q (Applied):** In the 6-year warrant backtest across 686 events (Market Cap > ₹500 cr), what were the 3-year and 5-year hit rates and average returns, and why do promoters issue warrants? **A:** 3Y hit rate was 72.4% (+145.3% avg / +58.7% median) and 5Y hit rate was 77.8% (+459.9% avg / +104.5% median). Promoters put 25% capital upfront with an 18-month conversion window (forfeited if unexercised), signaling either balance-sheet repair or an expected J-curve inflection (Stage 8).
- **Q:** What S-curve penetration threshold (cited from Rohit Chauhan) confirms an inflection in electric two-wheelers, and how should an investor map it? **A:** Sustained >10% share of total 2W registrations; investors should map "horizontal S-curves" across shunts, battery packs, chemicals, auto parts, and advanced plastics rather than only OEMs (Stage 4).
- **Q (Applied):** Trace the three Behind-the-Meter (BTM) power pathways solving the 5-year US data-center grid bottleneck to their Indian value-chain suppliers. **A:** (1) CCPP (GE Vernova/Siemens/Mitsubishi) → DEE Piping [?] (HRSG pipes) & Triveni Turbines (steam turbines); (2) SOFC (Bloom Energy) → MTAR [?]; (3) Modular 5×5 MW Gas Engines (INNIO/Vistra/Solar Turbines) → TD Power (generators); plus Welspun Corp for Permian Basin SAW gas pipelines (Stage 5).
- **Q:** Why can percentage EBITDA margins be misleading for KSH International, and what metric should be tracked instead? **A:** Because copper is a full pass-through, rising copper prices create a denominator effect that lowers % margin optics; investors should track absolute EBITDA per tonne (which rose from ₹59,630 in Q4 FY25 to ₹74,018 in Q4 FY26) (Stage 9).
- **Q (Applied):** In the Spirits Value Pyramid, why does a 5% mix shift toward Prestige+ spirits add 100–200 bps to OPM? **A:** Because excise duty is largely fixed while premium price realization is uncapped, yielding 2–4x higher EBITDA per case (₹25–40 per ₹100 revenue vs. ₹8–10 in Economy) (Stage 9).
- **Q:** What is the difference between StockScans' *Explosive Growth* and *Buoyant Growth* scans, and what does *Scan Match* do? **A:** *Explosive Growth* filters >20% annual sales growth; *Buoyant Growth* requires both >20% sales and >20% PAT growth; *Scan Match* intersects fundamental and technical scans and ranks the top ~100 overlapping stocks (Stage 10).
- **Q:** Which red-flag keywords should be scanned inside Annual Reports to catch accounting or governance issues without reading 500 pages? **A:** *Qualified Opinion*, *Adverse Opinion*, *Disclaimer of Opinion*, *Key Audit Matter*, *We did not audit*, *subsidiaries located outside India*, *Emphasis of Matter*, *Auditor remuneration*, *Fraudulent practices*, SEBI show-cause notices, and repayments (Stage 11).
- **Q:** How does the 2-4-6-8 portfolio sizing framework allocate capital across a 20–25 stock portfolio? **A:** 2% (low conviction), 4% (OK), 6% (good), and 8% (very high conviction), with most positions clustered at 4–6% (Stage 12).

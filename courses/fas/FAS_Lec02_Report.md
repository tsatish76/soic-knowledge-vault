## 2. KEY LEARNINGS
- **Automation Over Manual Screening:** Manually screening ~5,000 listed Indian businesses takes roughly 4 months; automated scanners (StockScans) compress this to minutes so investor time goes into reading and researching businesses (`[0:00:34–0:01:12]`).
- **Industry-Level Relative Strength (Mother-and-Kittens Rule):** Leading stocks and industries break into new all-time highs while broad indices (Nifty 50, Nifty 500) remain range-bound in a channel; per Stan Weinstein's Stage 2 rule, when the industry "mother" moves into Stage 2, constituent stocks follow (`[0:01:49–0:05:47]`).
- **RSI vs. RS Distinction:** RSI measures a stock's own internal momentum (e.g., improving one's own marks from 20 → 30 → 40), whereas Relative Strength (RS) measures outperformance relative to peers/benchmarks (scoring 35–45 compared to classmates) (`[0:07:07–0:08:16]`).
- **Custom Industry Index Construction:** Build custom indices (Market Cap > ₹1,000 cr) from stocks trading above EMA/VSTOP and within <20% of 52-week highs to spot Stage 1 → Stage 2 industry inflections early, and audit every portfolio holding against its industry stage (`[0:10:00–0:13:16]`).
- **Fundamental Earnings Breakout Scan:** Filter for stocks with Market Cap ₹1,000–20,000 cr whose prior 4 quarters of PAT were range-bound within a 10% (`1.10`) band and whose latest quarter PAT jumped >30% (narrowing 320 companies to 13) (`[0:14:23–0:18:30]`).
- **Two-Check Rule for Warrant Issues:** Never buy warrant issues blindly; verify (1) why capital is being raised (capex/expansion vs. general corporate use) and who is participating (promoters vs. external investors), and (2) whether the industry has Stage 2 tailwinds (`[0:26:47–0:29:23]`).
- **Consistent Margin Expansion & PAT Growth Scans:** Screen for 3–4 consecutive quarters of ≥10% sequential OPM expansion (with latest OPM >1% to exclude banks) or 4 consecutive quarters of >10% PAT growth (preferring PAT over EPS to avoid split/bonus distortion) (`[0:42:51–0:44:11]`, `[0:50:17–0:51:24]`).
- **Loss-to-Profit Turnaround on Bombed-Out IPOs:** Screen companies listed <600 days (~2 years) that posted negative PAT in the prior/preceding-year quarter and >₹1 cr profit in the latest quarter, then automate weekly email alerts (`[0:52:32–0:54:51]`).
- **FII+DII Stake Increase Needs Stage 2 Context:** Backtesting `FII + DII stake increase > 2%` (Market Cap > ₹2,500 cr) 1 year back yielded 54 stocks, of which only 25 outperformed (~46%)—proving institutional buying is only half the story without Stage 2 and fundamental confirmation (`[0:59:56–1:02:15]`).
- **Volume + Earnings + P2P Convergence:** Combining `Close > 30W EMA`, `10D Volume > 1.20 × 50D Volume`, `RS > 2%`, and `PAT growth > 20%` narrows 5,000 stocks to 83; then run Peer-to-Peer (P2P) comparison to pick the industry peer with the strongest earnings growth (`[1:17:31–1:21:46]`).

## FAS-Lec02 – Stage 1: Why Screening Matters & Industry-Level Relative Strength  [0:00:05–0:06:12]  [TVGP: Theme, Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
Why is automated stock screening essential across a 5,000-stock universe, and why must investors evaluate industry-level Relative Strength and Stage 2 trends before picking individual stocks?
### b) Detailed explanation
With new IPOs arriving every year, nearly 5,000 businesses are listed in the Indian stock market [0:00:34–0:01:12; Slide 2]. Manually screening 5,000 companies one by one would take roughly four months [0:01:12–0:01:49]. Automated screening platforms (such as StockScans) compress this filtering step to minutes, acting as leverage so the investor can spend their time reading and researching the shortlisted businesses rather than hunting through raw lists [0:00:34–0:01:49]. Basic scans (such as simple margin expansion or relative strength) are covered in the Saturday weekly StockScans videos (43rd video completed out of 52 per year), whereas this session goes deeper into Relative Strength and early Stage 2 identification [0:01:12–0:02:23].

To understand why industry-level Relative Strength comes first, the speaker cites Mark Minervini's principle on market turning points [0:01:49–0:02:23; Slide 7]:

- Top-performing stocks lead the broader market averages at important turning points [0:01:49–0:02:23; Slide 7].
- As a bear market bottoms, leading stocks ("the ones in their running shoes") break into new high ground while major indices are only starting to come off their lows and overall market sentiment and news remain bleak, negative, and cautionary [0:02:23–0:03:10; Slide 7].
- Only later, as the rally extends higher, does sentiment shift from fear to optimism [0:02:23–0:03:10; Slide 7].

Applying this to the live market, broad indices such as Nifty 50 and Nifty 500 are not making new all-time highs; instead, they are moving within a range-bound channel or band [0:02:23–0:03:54]. However, when the speaker inspects custom industry indices—such as Mining & Minerals, Shipping, and Chemicals Fluorine—those specific industry indices are hitting new all-time highs even while the broader market remains stuck in a channel [0:03:10–0:05:19].

Investors should focus on industries making new all-time highs in **Stage 2** rather than Stage 4 [0:04:34–0:05:19]. Grounding this in Stan Weinstein's framework (*Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*, Slide 4), when an industry moves into Stage 2, its constituent stocks follow [0:05:19–0:05:47]. The speaker illustrates this with a childhood observation: when a mother cat walks forward, her kittens naturally follow behind her—the industry is the mother, and individual stocks in that industry are the kittens [0:05:19–0:05:47]. Furthermore, sector strength (12%) and industry group strength (37%) together influence roughly half (37% + 12%) of a stock's move [Slide 5].
### c) Examples & case studies

  - **Walkthrough:** By comparing broad benchmark charts against custom industry indices, the investor immediately sees where leadership and Stage 2 momentum reside before looking at individual tickers.
  - **Takeaway:** Do not wait for Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 to break out; track custom industry indices entering Stage 2 and making new highs first.

## FAS-Lec02 – Stage 2: The 5,000-to-Watchlist Funnel, RSI vs. RS & Sector Divergence  [0:06:12–0:10:00]  [TVGP: Theme]
### a) What this stage answers
How does an investor narrow 5,000 stocks into a research-ready watchlist without treating a scan as a buy signal, and what is the difference between RSI and Relative Strength (RS)?
### b) Detailed explanation
The speaker lays out a disciplined screening funnel to convert market noise into research-ready ideas [0:06:12–0:07:07; Slide 3]:

- **Universe:** Start with ~5,000+ listed companies.
- **Scan:** Apply technical parameters, fundamental parameters, and qualitative frameworks.
- **Verify & Research:** Read filings, charts, and data to understand the business.
- **Watchlist:** Add verified candidates to a tracking list before building conviction.

The speaker emphasizes that the platform is called **StockScans**, not "StockBuy.com" or "StockBuy.in"—a scan only surfaces an idea and never replaces human judgment, business quality checks, valuation, or management assessment [0:06:12–0:07:07; Slide 3].

Next, the speaker clarifies a frequent confusion: **RSI vs. RS** [0:07:07–0:08:16; Slide 6]. Referencing a past class where Ishmohit taught RS (Relative Strength) and a student messaged an hour later about "RSI 40," the speaker explains the distinction using a school marks analogy [0:07:07–0:08:16]:

- **RSI (Relative Strength Index):** Measures a stock's *own internal momentum* (speed and change of its own price movement). In the analogy, if a student scores 20 marks in one test, 30 in the next, and 40 (or 20 → 40 → 60) after studying harder, their own score improved—that is internal momentum (RSI) [0:07:35–0:08:16].
- **RS (Relative Strength):** Measures *leadership versus peers or a benchmark*. In the analogy, if the student used to score 20 compared to classmates and now scores 35 or 45 relative to classmates, their marks show relative outperformance—that is Relative Strength (RS) [0:07:35–0:08:16].

When a custom industry index is trending up in Stage 2 ("mother moving forward, children following"), the probability of constituent stocks making new all-time highs and generating returns is high [0:08:16–0:08:51]. Comparing strong vs. weak industries from September 2024 (when the market was at a top) illustrates how industry context changes the entire research question [0:08:51–0:10:00; Slides 10, 12]: strong industries exhibit supportive narratives, government capex tailwinds, and outperforming constituents, whereas weak industries drag down their constituents.
### c) Examples & case studies
- **Example: Shipping Index (+50%) vs. Footwear Index (-37%)**  [0:08:51–0:10:00] [Slides 10, 12]
  - **Context:** Comparing two custom industry indices from the September 2024 market peak to show how industry Relative Strength drives constituent stock performance.

  - **Takeaway:** Always compare a stock against its industry index and peers; when the industry has Relative Strength and hits Stage 2 / all-time high territory, its businesses tend to perform similarly.

## FAS-Lec02 – Stage 4: Fundamental Earnings Breakout Scan  [0:13:16–0:19:08]  [TVGP: Growth]
### a) What this stage answers
How do you translate Mark Minervini's "earnings breakout" concept into an automated fundamental scan on StockScans to catch companies breaking out of multi-quarter profit stagnation?
### b) Detailed explanation
Breakouts do not happen only in stock prices—they happen in **earnings** too [0:13:16–0:13:43; Slide 14]. Quoting Mark Minervini (*Look for a Breakout Year*, Slide 14):

- Look back 2 to 4 years (or more) to a record year and check if current earnings are breaking out above the previous trend [0:13:16–0:13:43; Slide 14].
- Even when earnings suddenly break out to the upside from a range that stabilized over several years, it can be fairly significant [0:13:16–0:13:43].
- Check the upcoming 1–2 quarters and next fiscal year to verify if earnings acceleration is expected to continue; for instance, a company growing 12% a year that suddenly accelerates to 40% and then 100% can be a hot prospect [0:13:43–0:14:23; Slide 14].

To convert this book concept into a StockScans query, the speaker builds the **Earnings Breakout** scan [0:14:23–0:16:40]:

- **4-Quarter Range-Bound PAT:** Compare the prior 4 quarters of Profit After Tax (PAT) sequentially using a `1.10` (10%) band—specifically comparing PAT 3 quarters/years back vs. preceding quarter (Q4 vs. Q3, Q3 vs. Q2, Q2 vs. Q1) so that all four prior quarters stayed within a tight ~10% range [0:14:52–0:15:54].

## 7. GLOSSARY
- **StockScans:** Automated Indian equity screening and announcement-search platform used by the speaker to narrow ~5,000 listed companies into research watchlists ("StockScans, not StockBuy") [0:00:34–0:01:12, 0:06:12–0:07:07].
- **Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3 / Stage 4 (Stan Weinstein):** Market cycle stages—Stage 1 (basing/consolidation), Stage 2 (advancing uptrend), Stage 3 (topping/distribution), Stage 4 (declining downtrend) [0:04:34–0:05:47, 0:11:37–0:13:16].
- **Relative Strength (RS):** A stock or industry's price performance relative to a benchmark or peers (Stock Price ÷ Market Average); distinct from RSI [0:05:19, 0:07:07–0:08:16].
- **RSI (Relative Strength Index):** Momentum oscillator measuring the speed and change of a stock's own price movements (internal momentum) [0:07:07–0:08:16].
- **VSTOP (Volatility Stop):** Trend/volatility indicator showing whether volatility is expanding to the upside (positive VSTOP) or downside (negative VSTOP) [0:10:38, 0:26:18].
- **30-Week EMA:** 30-week Exponential Moving Average used as a key Stage 2 trendline filter [0:25:03, 0:33:31, 1:17:31].
- **Market Breadth (200D EMA):** Percentage of listed stocks trading above their 200-day EMA (cited at 9–15% during weak breadth phases) [0:35:26–0:35:34].
- **PAT (Profit After Tax):** Net profit metric preferred over EPS in consistency scans because PAT is not distorted by stock splits or bonus issues [0:50:54–0:51:24].
- **OPM (Operating Profit Margin):** Operating profit as a percentage of sales; filtered at > 1% in OPM expansion scans to exclude banking stocks [0:41:01–0:43:29].
- **Warrant Issue / Preferential Allotment:** Capital raise where investors/promoters typically pay 25% upfront and the balance upon conversion; tracked via Regulation 30 (LODR) filings [0:26:47–0:27:20, Slide 26].
- **Quick Search vs. Full Search:** In StockScans Announcement Scans, Quick Search scans only announcement headlines/titles, whereas Full Search scans inside attached PDF documents and investor presentations [0:32:31, 1:12:48–1:13:24].
- **`@Company` Hack:** Typing `@<Company>` alongside keywords like `acquisition` in Announcement Scans to pull a company's full 8–12 quarter historical track record [1:13:47–1:14:47].
- **Result Tracker:** Pre-built StockScans screen catching > 20% YoY margin expansion, > 10% sales growth, and > 6% latest OPM [0:45:45–0:46:21].
- **SSSG (Same-Store Sales Growth):** Retail store-level revenue growth metric noted in BlueStone Jewellery concall notes [0:54:51–0:55:23].
- **SAST Disclosure:** Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers filing surfaced in keyword scans [Slide 46].
- **RPT (Related Party Transaction):** Transaction between related entities (e.g., Vilas Transcore acquiring Fortuner Engineering via equity share swap) [1:12:48–1:13:47].
- **Swap Ratio:** Share exchange ratio in a merger (e.g., 56 Sequent Scientific shares per 100 Viyash Life Sciences shares) [1:16:18–1:17:31].
- **P2P (Peer-to-Peer) Comparison:** Side-by-side technical (1Y RS) and fundamental (PAT growth, YoY revenue, EBITDA margin) comparison of up to 10 industry peers [1:19:15–1:22:18].
- **Golden Crossover:** Technical moving-average crossover ("Lollip G [?]" in STT) covered on the StockScans YouTube channel; used without definition in Q&A [1:22:59].
- **CDMO / HPP:** Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization / High Performance Products (Navin Fluorine business segments); used without full expansion [0:47:01–0:48:19, Slide 37].

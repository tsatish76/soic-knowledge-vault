## 2. KEY LEARNINGS
- **Markets move in packs (67% Group Move Rule):** Per William O'Neil's 1953–1993 study, **67% (2 out of 3)** of big stock winners are part of broader industry group moves; finding a strong business in a strong sector is the "holy grail," while a strong business in a weak sector (e.g., SRF, PI Industries post-Nov 2021) suffers prolonged time correction [00:23:57, 00:31:53–00:32:27, 00:50:17–00:51:23].
- **Technicals & liquidity are the catalyst for cheap valuations:** Valuation alone only tells you how far a stock can go, not when it will move (e.g., Nifty PSU Banks stayed a value trap from 2014 until 2021); wait for liquidity and Stage 2 technicals to trigger re-rating [00:33:42–00:36:17].
- **Custom equi-weighted sector indices on TradingView:** Build equal-weighted sector baskets using explicit brackets and division—e.g., `(5*NAVINFLUOR + 5*SRF + 5*FLUOROCHEM)/15`—so high-priced stocks do not distort index weighting [00:59:33–01:00:45].
- **Ratio charts for inter-sector rotation:** Plot `Numerator ÷ Denominator` (e.g., `Nifty Private Bank / Nifty PSU Bank`, `Nifty Pharma / Nifty`, `BSE Smallcap / Sensex`) and set a **30-weekly EMA alert** on the ratio chart to catch multi-year leadership shifts early [01:01:57–01:11:45, 01:46:35–01:50:08].
- **Economic cycle tracking via macro & corporate profit/GDP:** Check GST growth, GDP (~7.2%), and corporate profits as % of GDP (4.8% vs. 3.7% long-term average and 5.2% prior cycle peak; Nifty 500 PAT growing 34.5% in 2021–24 vs. 30% in 2003–08) to position in expansion-led sectors [01:50:57–01:57:56].
- **Market breadth extremes for index allocation:** On Chartink's free SOIC Market Temperature Check, when stocks above 200 EMA/SMA drop to **12%–15%** (e.g., 14.5% on 4 June at Nifty P/E 18.5), fear peaks and index SIP/lump-sum odds are highest; >75%–80% signals strong market-wide momentum [02:16:04–02:21:45].
- **Stock Scans + Tijori + Fundamentals filter:** Use SOIC Stock Scans (monthly/weekly V-Stop positive + 3-month RS outperformance) and Tijori Market Monitor to spot emerging sectors, then verify fundamentals (e.g., avoiding bearings at 62x–88x P/E with 10–15% growth and promoter selling) [02:01:33–02:14:20].
- **Relative Rotation Graphs (RRG) on weekly/monthly timeframes:** Track sectors moving from **Improving → Leading** on **10-week** or **3–6 month** RRG charts while ignoring noisy daily charts [02:22:10–02:33:36].
- **Remove "value vs. momentum" tags & respect exit rules:** Buying undervalued assets and selling when peak momentum breaks or intrinsic value is reached is true investing—being "long-term for the sake of being long-term" through Stage 4 downtrends is a costly mistake [02:46:52–02:53:48].

Markets run in groups—when a sector or sub-sector enters an uptrend (or downtrend), peer companies inside that group tend to exhibit nearly identical chart structures, volume surges, and 30-weekly EMA breakouts [00:02:13–00:02:56, 00:15:50–00:16:35]. - **The Holy Grail vs. Basket Approach:** Finding a **strong company in a strong sector** is the **holy grail of stock picking** [00:23:57–00:24:33]. Even if an investor cannot pinpoint the single best winner inside a strong theme (such as semiconductors or Bangalore micro-market real estate), identifying the strong sector allows playing it effectively through a **basket approach** (e.g., holding multiple leaders across the group) [00:21:14–00:24:33].

# BS-Lec08 – Stage 2: Relative Strength in Bear Markets, Price-Anchoring Bias & 67% Group Move Rule  [00:24:33–00:34:22]  [TVGP: Theme]
## a) What this stage answers
Why do certain sectors enter bull markets even during broader market bear phases, how does price-anchoring bias cause investors to exit winners too early, and what empirical evidence backs the 67% group-move rule?
## b) Detailed explanation
1. Relative Strength During Bear Markets (2018–2020):
Even during severe broader bear markets (such as 2018–2020), specific sectors quietly enter bull markets [00:24:33–00:27:46]. Using Relative Strength (RS) against the Nifty 500, investors can spot stocks and sectors making higher highs while the broader index struggles [00:25:18–00:27:46].

**2. Overcoming Price-Anchoring Bias:**
Speaker's view: retail and even value investors frequently suffer from **price-anchoring bias**—refusing to buy or selling too early simply because a stock's price "has gone up a lot" [00:27:46–00:31:53]. Instead of anchoring to past prices, investors should anchor to:

- **Earnings growth** and **2–3 year forward math** [00:28:43–00:29:17].
- **Guidance upgrades and business catalysts** (e.g., major project launches) [00:28:43–00:29:17].
- **Stage Analysis** (whether the stock remains in Stage 2 with reasonable valuations) [00:28:09–00:28:43].

Speaker's view: riding a bull market in a strong sector is psychologically much harder than sitting on the sidelines, because buyers keep stepping in at higher prices as earnings deliver [00:29:50–00:30:03].

**3. The 67% Group Move Rule (William O'Neil):**
Citing William O'Neil's study (*How to Make Money in Stocks*, analyzing the biggest winners from **1953 through 1993**), the speaker highlights a core rule: **67% (2 out of 3) of major stock market winners are part of broad industry group advances** [00:31:53–00:33:06] [Slide 6–7]. Because sector movement is the largest contributor to returns, even fundamental analysts cannot afford to ignore group moves [00:32:27–00:33:42].

**4. Stanley Druckenmiller on Liquidity vs. Valuation:**
Citing Stanley Druckenmiller (noted for a **31% CAGR** track record without a down year, and a major long position in Nvidia), the speaker explains that **valuation alone only tells you how far a stock can go** (e.g., it does not make sense to initiate at 100 P/E), **not when it will move** [00:33:42–00:34:22].

# BS-Lec08 – Stage 3: Valuation Traps vs. Liquidity Catalysts, US Winners & Active Sectoral Rotation  [00:34:22–00:47:11]  [TVGP: Valuation]
## a) What this stage answers
Why do cheap sectors remain value traps for years without technical/liquidity catalysts, what does historical US and Indian sector data reveal about cyclical leadership, and why does active sectoral rotation require disciplined exit rules?
## b) Detailed explanation
1. Valuation Tells You "How Far," Technicals & Liquidity Tell You "When":
Cheap valuations alone do not trigger a rally [00:34:22–00:36:17]. **Technicals and liquidity** are the primary catalysts that signal when an undervalued sector is finally being discovered by institutional money [00:34:22–00:34:59]. When a sector enters a ferocious **Stage 2**, bottom-up companies inside it also enter Stage 2, signaling investors to scale up allocations quickly [00:35:37–00:36:17].

**2. "There Is Always a Bull Market Somewhere" (1953–1994 US Study):**
Drawing from William O'Neil's How to Make Money in Stocks (1953–1994) [Slide 4], every bull market has distinct leading industry groups [00:37:17–00:38:49]. Investors fall into three categories: (1) those participating in the current bull market (happy), (2) those not participating (acceptable), and (3) those not participating and complaining—who need to fix their process [00:38:49–00:39:51].

**3. Active Sectoral Rotation vs. Buy-and-Forget:**
This session focuses on **active investing and sectoral rotation**, not 10–15 year buy-and-forget investing [00:39:51–00:41:01]. Most companies—especially in cyclical sectors like hotels—cannot be held passively through 16–17 year downcycles [00:39:51–00:42:30].

**4. SOIC Investor Pulse Sector Heatmap & Mean Reversion:**
Presenting data from **SOIC Investor Pulse** [Slide 17], the speaker tracks the **percentage of stocks in each sector index outperforming the broader index each year** (values **> 70%** highlighted green for strong sector bull runs) [00:42:30–00:43:09].

- **True Bear Market Cadence:** In Indian markets, every **5–7 years** typically brings **~2 true bear market years** (e.g., **2010–2011** and **2018–2019**), where almost no sectors outperform [00:43:09–00:43:45]. Across global markets, 4–5 bull years are usually followed by 2 down years where indices fall 15%–20% and individual stocks drop 30%–50% [00:43:45–00:44:28].
- **Return Concentration & Mean Reversion:** Long-term CAGRs are ~13%–14% for Nifty, ~14%–15% for NSE 500 (Nifty 500), and ~18%–19% for BSE Smallcap, with bulk returns arriving in bursts [00:44:28–00:45:30]. If a portfolio compounds at 100%–120% CAGR in 2 years (e.g., a 10x), it is far above the mean and faces mean reversion—making **technical exit systems** (on monthly charts) essential to protect gains when earnings slow [00:43:45–00:45:52].

## 7. GLOSSARY
- **30-Weekly EMA (30W EMA / 30 WMA):** A 30-week exponential (or weighted) moving average used on weekly charts to identify Stage 2 uptrends, ratio-chart breakouts, and normal-trend exit levels [00:07:32, 01:10:30, 02:17:20, 02:35:40].
- **10-Weekly EMA (10W EMA):** A faster 10-week exponential moving average used to tighten trailing exits when a stock enters steep momentum [02:35:40–02:37:50].
- **AIF (Alternative Investment Fund):** Pooled investment vehicle regulated by SEBI; referenced via the speaker's newly approved **Persistence Capital** AIF (minimum ₹1 crore investment, listed and unlisted equities, no F&O) [02:50:37–02:51:09, 02:56:11–02:57:21].
- **Basket Approach:** Holding multiple leading stocks across an identified strong sector when picking the single best winner is difficult [00:21:14–00:24:33].
- **Bottom-Up Stock Picking:** Analyzing individual company fundamentals, quality, and governance (used to avoid fraud/weak businesses after top-down sector selection) [00:22:02–00:22:22].
- **Chartink Market Temperature Check:** A free SOIC breadth dashboard on Chartink tracking % of stocks above 200 EMA/SMA, 50 EMA, 20 EMA/SMA, and sectoral % distance from 30W EMA [02:16:12–02:22:10].
- **Corporate Profit to GDP:** Total corporate net profit (e.g., Nifty-500 PAT) expressed as a percentage of nominal GDP to gauge where the economy sits in the profit cycle [01:55:17–01:57:14].
- **Equi-Weighted Custom Index:** A TradingView formula using explicit brackets and equal multipliers (e.g., `(5*A + 5*B + 5*C)/15`) so high nominal share prices do not skew sub-sector performance [00:59:33–01:01:57].
- **Forced Long-Term Investing:** Getting trapped holding an overvalued stock (e.g., 100x P/E) for 5+ years after growth slows because of failing to apply an exit system [00:53:19–00:54:00, 02:52:03].
- **Group Move:** Synchronized price and volume movement across peer stocks in the same industry or sub-sector [00:02:13–00:02:56, 00:31:53].
- **Holy Grail of Stock Picking:** Finding a fundamentally strong company inside a strong, trending sector [00:23:57–00:24:33].
- **HUF (Hindu Undivided Family):** Family tax/investment entity mentioned as a way 3–4 family members can pool capital to meet the ₹1 crore AIF minimum [02:56:48–02:57:21].
- **Overhead Supply:** Prior resistance/trapped-buyer price levels above current price; speaker notes sustained high volume absorbs overhead supply [01:36:00].
- **P/E (Price-to-Earnings), P/B (Price-to-Book), P/S (Price-to-Sales):** Valuation multiples used to compare sectors against 10–15 year historical averages and check for "valuation juice" [00:54:00–00:59:33, 01:07:52–01:10:30, 01:58:41–02:00:48].
- **Price-Anchoring Bias:** Behavioral bias of refusing to buy or selling winners prematurely solely because the stock price has risen relative to past prices [00:27:46–00:31:53].
- **QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement):** Capital-raising route for listed Indian companies; back-to-back QIPs at peak valuations signal sector overheating/froth [00:52:49–00:53:52].
- **Ratio Chart:** A chart plotting `Numerator ÷ Denominator` (e.g., `NIFTYPVTBANK/CNXPSUBANK`) to track relative outperformance between two indices or assets [01:01:57–01:02:34].
- **Relative Rotation Graph (RRG):** A four-quadrant chart (Improving, Leading, Weakening, Lagging) rotating clockwise to visualize sector relative strength and momentum against a benchmark (`$CNX500`) [02:22:10–02:30:30].
- **Relative Strength (RS):** Measuring how a stock or sector performs relative to a benchmark index (such as Nifty 500) [00:25:18–00:27:46].
- **Reversion to Mean:** Tendency for extreme outperformance (e.g., 100%–120% CAGR bursts) or prolonged underperformance (e.g., Private Banks vs. Nifty) to revert toward long-term averages [00:44:28–00:45:52, 01:32:58–01:33:21].
- **ROCE (Return on Capital Employed):** Used without definition [01:43:25].
